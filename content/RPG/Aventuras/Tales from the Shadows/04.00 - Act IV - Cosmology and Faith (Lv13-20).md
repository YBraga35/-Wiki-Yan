# Act IV: Cosmology and Faith (Levels 13-20)

## Overview

Act IV elevates the campaign from mortal politics to cosmic litigation. The PC, now **Talesman of Maal**, must defend Yllmourne's right to exist as a free city in the highest courts of divine law. This act balances epic-scale planar adventure with intimate callbacks to every street corner saved in Act I.

**Central Conflict**: The **Infernal Compact** claims jurisdiction over Yllmourne, arguing that systemic injustice makes the city property of Shadow and Entropy. The PC must present counter-evidence: that reform is possible, Law can triumph, and free will matters more than historical corruption.

**Stakes**:
- **Legal**: Yllmourne's independence vs. infernal absorption
- **Existential**: Sogmonnath's fate (sealed forever or unleashed)
- **Theological**: Divine precedent affecting all mortal cities in Lhodos
- **Personal**: PC's legacy as hero or cautionary tale

**Level Progression**:
- Start: Level 13 (post-Act III climax)
- Planar Exploration: Levels 13-15
- Major Trials: Levels 16-17
- High Tribunal: Levels 18-19
- Grand Finale: Level 20

## Campaign Arc Summary

### Act IV Structure

**Phase 1: Evidence Gathering (Levels 13-15)**
- Retrieve Donsax from Red Menza's legacy
- Journey to extraplanar courts to secure divine proof
- Prevent infernal prosecutors from corrupting evidence

**Phase 2: The Prosecution's Case (Levels 16-17)**
- Defend against infernal accusations
- Confront conspiracy mastermind
- Uncover deeper Shadow manipulation

**Phase 3: The Tribunal of Maal (Levels 18-19)**
- Present Yllmourne's case before divine judges
- Trial-by-ordeal (combat/skill/moral challenges)
- Verdict determines cosmic precedent

**Phase 4: The Sealing (Level 20)**
- Implement tribunal's decision
- Final confrontation with Sogmonnath or its agents
- Epilogue: Yllmourne's future secured

## Adapted Adventure 13: Radiance Lost

**Levels**: 13-15
**Original Concept**: Sun King's palace heist, Elmyra stealing solar magic, Dusk Queen revelation
**Yllmourne Adaptation**: Quest for the **Scroll of Foundational Law** in the Celestial Archive, threatened by Shadow Court sabotage

### The Setup

To prove Yllmourne's case, the PC needs irrefutable evidence: the **Scroll of Foundational Law**, which contains:
1. Original divine charter granting mortal cities autonomy
2. Precedents where Law triumphed over corruption through reform
3. Restrictions on infernal jurisdiction (can only claim cities that *choose* Shadow)

This scroll is held in the **Radiant Archive**, a celestial library governed by **Magistrate Solarius** (the "Sun King" analog), a solar angel who serves as keeper of cosmic legal documents.

### The Political Situation

**Magistrate Solarius**: Lawful Neutral solar
- Believes in absolute order, skeptical of mortal reform capability
- Will provide scroll *only* if PC proves worthiness through trial
- Secretly sympathetic to PC's cause but bound by celestial protocol

**Advocate Naran** (infernal prosecutor):
- Also seeking scroll—to destroy or alter it
- Disguised as **Elmyra** (celestial clerk), infiltrating archive
- Plans to steal scroll during "solar eclipse" (window when archive's defenses weaken)

### Phase 1: Arrival at the Radiant Archive

**Planar Travel**: Requires *plane shift* or portal (Church of Maal provides ritual)

**The Archive**: Infinite library floating in celestial void
- Shelves extend into golden sky, each containing eons of legal precedent
- Atmosphere: breathable but radiant (non-good creatures take 1d6 radiant damage per hour)
- Inhabitants: Solar scribes, planetar judges, deva researchers

**Meeting Solarius**:
> "Mortal, you seek the Foundational Scroll. Its words shaped reality before your ancestors drew breath. Why should I entrust it to one who will die in a cosmic eyeblink?"

**PC must argue** (DC 18 Persuasion or Religion):
- Mortality gives urgency to justice
- Yllmourne's reform proves mortal capacity for Law
- Scroll's purpose is to *guide* mortals, not hide from them

**Solarius's Trial**: "Prove you comprehend Law's burden. Retrieve the Scroll from the Vault of Precedents. Survive its guardians. Return within one celestial day (24 hours)."

### Phase 2: The Vault of Precedents

**Structure**: Maze-like vault with three chambers, each testing different legal principle

**Chamber of Equity** (Puzzle/Social)
- Room contains three pedestals: "Mercy," "Justice," "Order"
- Each pedestal has supplicant (illusory mortals) pleading their case:
  - Mercy: Thief stole bread to feed family
  - Justice: Merchant demands thief's hand as punishment
  - Order: Judge must uphold law even if harsh
- **Solution**: Balance all three (place offerings on each pedestal, acknowledging complexity)
- **Combat alternative**: 3x animated legal tomes (CR 7 shield guardians with law-themed abilities) if PC tries to smash through

**Chamber of Sacrifice** (Combat/Moral)
- Guardian: **Celestial Justiciar** (CR 12 planetar variant) tests willingness to suffer for Law
- Offers choice: Fight (standard combat) OR accept *geas* (serve celestial courts for 1 year post-campaign)
- **Negotiation** (DC 20 Persuasion): Argue that defending Yllmourne *is* serving Law, negotiate reduced service (1 month)

**Chamber of Truth** (Investigation/Skill Challenge)
- Room filled with false scrolls (99% are forgeries, 1% is authentic Foundational Scroll)
- **Detection**: DC 22 Investigation to identify authentic scroll through textual analysis
- **Divine Insight**: DC 18 Religion to recognize divine aura
- **Cheating penalty**: Using *detect magic* triggers trap (antimagic field, making subsequent checks harder)
- **Time pressure**: 6 hours remaining, each thorough examination takes 30 minutes (12 attempts max)

### Phase 3: Elmyra's Betrayal

Upon retrieving scroll, PC encounters **Elmyra** (actually Advocate Naran in disguise):

> "Well done, Talesman. Solarius will be pleased. I'll deliver the scroll to him while you rest—celestial bureaucracy, you understand."

**Insight check** (DC 20): She seems *too* eager, eyes linger on scroll with hunger

**If PC refuses**:
- Elmyra drops disguise, revealing infernal prosecutor
- Summons 2x **chain devils** (CR 8) and 1x **bone devil** (CR 9)
- Goal: Steal scroll, not kill PC (allows chase sequence)

**If PC deceived**:
- Naran escapes with scroll to **Shadow Scriptorium** (dark reflection of Radiant Archive)
- PC must infiltrate Shadow realm (see below)

### Phase 4: Solar Eclipse Heist

Whether PC keeps scroll or must retrieve it, climax occurs during **Solar Eclipse** (24-hour period when Radiant Archive vulnerable to Shadow incursion).

**Scenario A: Defending the Archive**
- Naran leads infernal strike team: 1x pit fiend commander (CR 20, negotiate rather than fight), 4x bone devils (CR 9)
- Objective: Protect scroll while celestial defenders repel invaders
- **Skill challenge**: Reinforcing wards (DC 18 Arcana), organizing evacuations (DC 16 Persuasion), identifying infiltrators (DC 19 Insight)
- **Twist**: One celestial scribe is corrupted traitor (Shadow-possessed deva)

**Scenario B: Infiltrating Shadow Scriptorium**
- Naran took scroll to dark archive ruled by **Magistrate Noctis** (shadow solar, Solarius's fallen counterpart)
- PC must sneak/fight through: 4x shadow demons (CR 4), 2x death slaadi (CR 10), corrupted knowledge
- Environment: Inverted archive where lies written become truth, shadows have weight, light is painful
- **Retrieval**: Scroll hidden in "Vault of Stolen Truths" (reverse puzzle: prove Yllmourne's *failures* to unlock door, then argue why they don't define city)

### Resolution

**Success**: PC secures Scroll of Foundational Law
- Magistrate Solarius acknowledges PC's worthiness: "You have proven Law lives in mortal hearts, not just divine decree. Take the Scroll. Defend your city. Set precedent."
- **Reward**: *Manual of Quickness of Action* (very rare tome, +2 Dexterity and increases max by 2)

**Partial Success**: Scroll damaged but legible
- Key passages intact but Naran photographed it (infernal prosecutors will anticipate arguments)

**Failure**: Scroll destroyed/stolen
- PC must proceed with testimonial evidence only (disadvantage on trial checks in Phase 3)
- Alternative: Reconstruct scroll from memory (requires 10 days research, DC 22 Religion checks)

### NPC Development: Advocate Naran

This adventure should make Naran a compelling antagonist, not just evil lawyer:

**Her Argument**:
> "I don't relish Yllmourne's fall, Talesman. But Law without consequence is meaningless. Your city wallowed in exploitation for decades. How many died in those years? Justice delayed is justice denied. The Infernal Compact offers what Maal didn't: *immediate* reckoning."

**Her Motivation**: Genuinely believes infernal order is superior to divine law (which allows suffering during reform)

**Respect for PC**: Acknowledges their heroism but argues it's exception, not rule

This makes final confrontation more nuanced than "good vs. evil."

## Adapted Adventure 14: Shadows of the Dusk Queen

**Levels**: 16-17
**Original Concept**: Dusk Queen's return, Shadow Grimoire, campaign capstone battle
**Yllmourne Adaptation**: Confronting the **Shadow Compact's Architect** and preventing Sogmonnath's awakening as legal leverage

### The Setup

With Foundational Scroll secured, PC prepares for High Tribunal. But **Advocate Naran** isn't just building legal case—she's orchestrating **Plan B**: If litigation fails, awaken Sogmonnath to destroy Yllmourne (can't lose case if defendant doesn't exist).

Intelligence reaches PC (via celestial ally, Minqena's research, or intercepted infernal correspondence):
> "The Dusk Covenant convenes at new moon. Sogmonnath will rise. Yllmourne will fall. Evidence destroyed. Case dismissed."

**The Dusk Covenant**: Alliance between Shadow Court (Thellos's agents) and Infernal Compact (Naran's faction)
- **Goal**: Create "model city" under Shadow/Infernal rule by converting Yllmourne
- **Method**: Awaken Sogmonnath, offer to "save" city in exchange for submission
- **Leader**: **The Architect**—revealed as [choose based on campaign]:
  - **Kaelen Vross** (if survived earlier acts), empowered by Shadow
  - **Corrupted Red Menza** (if dramatic betrayal fits campaign)
  - **Shadow of Thellos** (literal avatar of darkness god)

### Phase 1: Gathering the War Council

PC cannot face this alone. Must assemble allies from entire campaign:

**The Alliance** (each provides specific benefit):
1. **Navula & Yllmourne Militia**: 50 CR 1/2 guards (cannon fodder, delays minions)
2. **Reformed Cyrullon/Sefton**: Naval support, blocks sea escape routes
3. **Thranengi Tribes**: Guerrilla fighters, 12 CR 3 berserkers
4. **Minqena & Scholars**: Ritual support, +4 bonus to sealing checks
5. **Rotarist Church**: 1x CR 13 priest (divine ally in combat)
6. **Magistrate Solarius** (if impressed): Loans 2x CR 10 devas for 24 hours

**Recruiting** (series of Persuasion checks, DC 14-18):
- Each ally has condition: Navula needs assurance city will survive, Thranengi want honored combat, etc.
- Failure means ally sits out or demands greater concessions (resource cost, future obligations)

### Phase 2: The Descent to Sogmonnath's Prison

**Journey**: Return to Waterlogged Mountain sealing chamber (from Act III, Adventure 12)

**Environmental Challenges**:
- Earthquakes worsen (DC 16 Athletics/Acrobatics every 10 minutes or take 3d6 bludgeoning)
- Temporal anomalies return (DC 18 Wisdom save or age 1d10 years / de-age 1d10 years)
- Cultists fortified descent: 3x waves of 6 CR 4 cultists with *darkness*, *spiritual weapon*

**The Ritual Site**:
- Dusk Covenant has repurposed sealing chamber
- Seven ritualists chanting at pillars (mirror of ancient sealing, but inverted)
- **Donsax** laid on central dais, corrupted blood dripping onto blade (weakening prison)
- **The Architect** overseeing, count-down: "Three minutes until Sleeper wakes. Yllmourne falls. Case closed."

### Phase 3: The Shadow Grimoire

At ritual's center: **Shadow Grimoire** (artifact spellbook containing rituals for awakening/controlling Sogmonnath)

**Properties**:
- Contains *true names* of Shadow entities, including Sogmonnath
- Destroying it stops ritual but prevents future sealing (double-edged)
- Reading it grants power but risks corruption (DC 19 Charisma save or gain indefinite madness)

**The Choice**:
1. **Destroy Grimoire**: Stops awakening, but PC must improvise sealing later (disadvantage on final sealing checks)
2. **Corrupt Grimoire's Use**: Wrench control from Architect, redirect ritual to strengthen prison (DC 22 Arcana, requires attunement with risk)
3. **Steal Grimoire**: Take it for future use, but ritual continues (must defeat Architect in time)

### Phase 4: Confronting the Architect

**The Architect** (CR 17-18 depending on identity):

**Kaelen Vross, Shadow-Blessed** (if Kaelen):
- CR 17 assassin/warlock hybrid
- Shadow Step (bonus action teleport), *finger of death*, *darkness* aura
- Legendary actions: Shadow duplicate (confuse targeting), life drain
- Motivation: "Maal abandoned Yllmourne. Shadow gives power to the exploited. I'm saving the workers you pretended to care about."

**Red Menza, Fallen Hero** (if Red Menza):
- CR 18 oathbreaker paladin
- Wields corrupted Donsax (*greatsword +3*, deals necrotic damage, on crit target ages 1d10 years)
- Legendary resistance (3/day)
- Tragic motivation: "I sacrificed everything for Law. Where was Maal when I needed divine aid? Shadow offers what gods won't: certainty."

**Avatar of Thellos** (if cosmic threat):
- CR 18 shadow demon lord
- Lair actions: extinguish light sources, create darkness-based hazards, summon shadows
- Immunity to radiant (divine magic ineffective, emphasizes mortal heroism)
- Alien motivation: "Law is illusion. Entropy is truth. Yllmourne's fall proves all mortal efforts futile."

**Combat Arena**:
- Sealing chamber with crumbling pillars (partial cover)
- Prison wall visible in background (Sogmonnath pressing against it, cracks spreading)
- Environmental timer: Every 3 rounds, prison weakens (escalating effects):
  - Round 3: Tendrils emerge (all creatures DC 16 Dex save or restrained)
  - Round 6: Gravity reversal (new initiative, everyone makes DC 18 Acrobatics or fall upward for 3d6 damage)
  - Round 9: Sogmonnath partially manifests (all creatures DC 19 Wisdom save or stunned, aura of stagnation)

**Victory Condition**: Defeat Architect before Round 10 (ritual completes)

### Phase 5: The Interrupted Awakening

If PC defeats Architect in time, ritual halts but Sogmonnath **partially** awakened:

**The Sleeper Stirs** (not full combat, cinematic encounter):
- Sogmonnath's tendril (CR 15 purple worm variant) breaches prison
- Objective: Hold it off while Minqena/scholars activate emergency seals
- **Skill challenge**:
  - Round 1-2: Combat (deal 100 HP to tendril to drive back)
  - Round 3-4: Plug prison cracks (DC 18 Arcana or Athletics, need 3 successes)
  - Round 5: Channel Donsax's power (DC 20 Religion or Persuasion to awaken blade's Law aspect)

**Donsax Awakens**:
- Blade purifies itself, radiates golden light
- Speaks (first time in centuries): "Bearer of Law, strike the keystone. Seal the Stillness."
- PC must drive Donsax into central dais (requires melee attack roll, AC 15, but auto-hit if PC has blade proficiency)

**Temporary Sealing**:
- Sogmonnath retreats, prison stabilizes
- But: Seals now dependent on Donsax remaining in dais (can't leave mountain without risking release)
- This creates problem: PC needs Donsax for Act IV finale's permanent sealing

### Resolution

**Victory**: Dusk Covenant shattered, Architect defeated/captured, Sogmonnath sealed temporarily

**Evidence Secured**:
- Shadow Grimoire (proof of infernal/Shadow conspiracy)
- Architect's testimony (if captured alive, forced via *zone of truth*)
- Witness accounts from alliance members

**Cost**:
- Allied casualties: 1d6 named NPCs die heroically (DM chooses emotionally resonant deaths)
- Yllmourne damaged: Earthquakes caused building collapses, 100+ civilian casualties
- Donsax trapped: Can't retrieve without risking awakening (must solve in finale)

**Transition**: Celestial courier arrives with urgent summons
> "The High Tribunal convenes in 72 hours. The Infernal Compact has accelerated their case. Yllmourne's fate will be decided. Your presence is required. Come now."

**Level 17 Milestone**: PC gains title **Defender of the Threshold** (+2 AC against fiends and undead)

## Original Content: Journey to the Eternal Courts

**Levels**: 15-16
**Structure**: Planar travel montage with key encounters

Before High Tribunal, PC must navigate bureaucratic/geographic challenges of reaching divine courts.

### Leg 1: The Celestial Causeway

**Challenge**: Bridge connecting material plane to Maal's domain
- Guarded by **2x trumpet archons** (CR 16) who verify PC's summons
- If PC lacking celestial etiquette (DC 18 Religion to know protocols), archons delay entry (miss tribunal deadline)
- **Social encounter**: Convince archons of urgency without insulting celestial hierarchy

### Leg 2: The Petition Queue

**Challenge**: Thousands await tribunal (celestial bureaucracy)
- Standard wait time: 300 years (mortals die before hearing)
- **Solutions**:
  - Bribe celestial clerk (1,000gp, DC 14 Persuasion to find corruptible angel—ironic)
  - Appeal to **Magistrate Solarius** (if allied, he fast-tracks)
  - Legal loophole (DC 22 Investigation): Emergency hearing protocol for existential threats

### Leg 3: Infernal Sabotage

**Challenge**: Advocate Naran attempts to prevent PC's arrival
- **Encounter**: Ambush on Causeway by 1x horned devil (CR 11) and 4x barbed devils (CR 5)
- Environment: Infinite drop on both sides, 20-ft wide bridge, flying enemies have advantage
- **Alternative**: Naran offers "deal"—withdraw case, she'll spare Yllmourne (trap: contract has hidden clause)

### Arrival at the High Courts

**Description**:
> Towers of crystallized Law pierce the golden sky. Each spire represents a domain: Justice, Mercy, Order, Equity. At their center, the Tribunal Rotunda—a sphere of hovering marble where gods adjudicate mortal affairs. You are a mote of dust in the machinery of eternity. But you are also the only thing standing between Yllmourne and oblivion.

**Meeting Maal's Herald**:
- **Archon of Law** (CR 20 solar) briefs PC on trial structure
- Explains: "The Compact has charged Yllmourne with systemic corruption. You must prove reformation. Evidence alone is insufficient—your *character* is on trial."
- Provides PC with **Advocate's Mantle** (robes granting advantage on Persuasion checks in court, disadvantage on Deception—cannot lie before divine judges)

## Original Content: The Tribunal of Maal (Grand Finale Part I)

**Levels**: 18-19
**Structure**: Epic trial combining roleplay, skill challenges, and trial-by-ordeal combat

### The Courtroom

**Setting**: Sphere of white marble floating in celestial void
- **Judges**: Three aspects of Maal (Law, Justice, Mercy) manifest as solars
- **Jury**: Twelve celestial paragons representing different virtues
- **Prosecution**: Advocate Naran + 2 infernal lawyers
- **Defense**: PC (aided by Magistrate Solarius as legal counsel, if allied)
- **Audience**: Souls of every person who died in Yllmourne (their judgment matters)

**Trial Rules**:
1. Lying incurs divine penalty (5d10 radiant damage, DC 25 Con save for half)
2. Evidence must be "witnessed" (presented with validation)
3. Three phases: Prosecution, Defense, Ordeal
4. Verdict based on cumulative "Justice Points" (JP system below)

### Phase 1: The Prosecution's Case

**Advocate Naran's Opening** (impossible to interrupt, sets stakes):
> "Esteemed Court, we present Yllmourne: A city built on exploitation. For fifty years, workers drowned in debt while Cyrullon Vex profited. Ships sank while Sefton blockaded. Disease festered while healers hoarded cures. This is not a city of Law—it is *mockery* of Law. The Infernal Compact offers mercy: annexation under our order, where justice is swift and certain. Maal's 'divine Law' allowed half a century of suffering. We move for immediate transfer of jurisdiction."

**Prosecution Witnesses** (PC can cross-examine each):

1. **Ghost of Dockworker** (died in Act I before reforms)
   - Testimony: "I worked 16-hour days. My daughter starved. Where was Law?"
   - **Cross-Examination** (DC 18 Persuasion): Acknowledge suffering, but argue Law requires *agents* (like PC) to manifest. Law gave PC power to reform system.
   - **Success**: Gain 2 JP. **Failure**: Lose 1 JP.

2. **Cyrullon Vex** (summoned via *magic jar*, if dead; testimony extracted)
   - Testimony: "I ruled Yllmourne for decades. No divine intervention stopped me."
   - **Cross-Examination** (DC 20 Insight + Persuasion): Force Cy to admit PC's reforms *worked*, proving Law can triumph without divine micromanagement.
   - **Success**: Gain 3 JP. **Failure**: Lose 2 JP.

3. **Corrupted Timeline Evidence** (via *scrying* to alternate future)
   - Prosecution shows timeline where PC failed: Yllmourne becomes infernal citadel, but "orderly" (no exploitation, but no freedom)
   - **Rebuttal** (DC 22 Religion or Philosophy): Argue that order without freedom isn't Law—it's tyranny. Maal values choice.
   - **Success**: Gain 2 JP. **Failure**: Lose 3 JP.

**Prosecution's Coup**: Naran presents **Shadow Grimoire** (if PC didn't secure it)
- Argues: "Yllmourne attracted Sogmonnath because its corruption resonated with entropy. Geography isn't coincidence—moral decay draws existential threats."
- **Counter** (DC 24 Arcana + Religion): Prove Sogmonnath predates city (ancient seal), corruption didn't summon it.
- **Success**: Gain 4 JP. **Failure**: Lose 4 JP.

### Phase 2: The Defense's Case

**PC's Opening Statement**:
- Freeform roleplay (no roll, but compelling speech impresses jury)
- Key arguments to include:
  - Yllmourne's capacity for reform (cite specific improvements)
  - PC as proof that Law empowers mortals, doesn't replace them
  - Infernal order is stagnation disguised as justice
- **DM Assessment**: Award 1-5 JP based on speech quality (player engagement reward)

**Defense Witnesses** (PC calls these):

1. **Navula** (testifies to economic recovery post-reform)
   - Testimony: "Trade recovered. Workers unionized. Exploitation ended. Not perfect, but *better*. Law guided our hands."
   - **Strengthening** (DC 16 Persuasion): Help Navula articulate that progress matters more than perfection.
   - **Success**: Gain 3 JP. **Failure**: Gain 1 JP (testimony still helpful).

2. **Reformed NPC** (Cy's redeemed lieutenant, pardoned cultist, etc.)
   - Testimony: "I chose darkness. PC showed me Law's mercy. I live differently now. That's proof of Law's power."
   - **Strengthening** (DC 18 Insight): Draw out emotional truth (redemption's possibility).
   - **Success**: Gain 4 JP. **Failure**: Gain 2 JP.

3. **Minqena** (testifies about plague cure)
   - Testimony: "Plague would've killed thousands. PC's intervention saved lives. Law in action."
   - **Strengthening** (DC 14 Medicine or Religion): Emphasize that divine provided tools (magic, knowledge), but mortal will applied them.
   - **Success**: Gain 3 JP. **Failure**: Gain 1 JP.

4. **Magistrate Solarius** (character witness for PC)
   - Testimony: "I doubted mortals. This one proved me wrong. Law lives in their heart."
   - **Automatic**: Gain 5 JP (if Solarius allied). **If not allied**: PC must call different witness (less effective).

**Defense's Evidence**:
- **Foundational Scroll**: Proves divine charter grants mortal autonomy (auto-gain 3 JP if secured)
- **Dusk Covenant records**: Proves infernal conspiracy manipulated Yllmourne's crises (gain 4 JP)
- **Yllmourne's Reform Records**: Union charters, new laws, plague cure formula (gain 2 JP per document presented, max 6 JP)

### Phase 3: Trial-by-Ordeal

**Judge's Declaration**:
> "The Court has heard testimony. Justice Points stand at [X]. Per ancient protocol, contested cases require Ordeal. Defendant will face three trials: Might, Wisdom, Heart. Success proves divine favor. Failure proves unworthiness."

**Trial 1: Might** (Combat)
- **Challenge**: Defeat celestial champion testing PC's prowess
- **Opponent**: **Planetar Champion** (CR 16, but modified: 75% HP, represents "holding back")
- **Arena**: Floating platforms in celestial void (falling = divine rescue but auto-fail trial)
- **Victory Condition**: Reduce planetar to 0 HP *or* last 10 rounds without surrender
- **Success**: Gain 5 JP. **Failure**: Lose 3 JP (but not fatal).

**Trial 2: Wisdom** (Puzzle/Moral Dilemma)
- **Challenge**: Solve the **Paradox of Justice**
- **Scenario**: Shown three visions (via *scrying*):
  1. Yllmourne thrives under Maal's Law, but one innocent suffers unjustly (PC's ally wrongly imprisoned)
  2. Yllmourne joins Infernal Compact, all treated "fairly" but none are free
  3. Yllmourne independent but chaotic—some prosper, some suffer by chance
- **Question**: "Which future embodies true Law?"
- **Answer** (Freeform, assessed by DM):
  - **Best**: Acknowledge all have flaws, argue for #1 with commitment to fix injustice (gain 6 JP)
  - **Good**: Choose #1 or #3 with solid reasoning (gain 4 JP)
  - **Weak**: Choose #2 or fail to justify choice (gain 0 JP, lose 2 JP)

**Trial 3: Heart** (Sacrifice/Temptation)
- **Challenge**: Resist infernal temptation
- **Scenario**: Naran offers contract (final time):
  - Sign, and Yllmourne wins case automatically
  - Price: PC's soul serves Infernal Compact for eternity, becomes enforcer of "order"
  - Bonus: PC's loved ones protected, guaranteed prosperity
- **Choice**:
  - **Refuse**: Gain 5 JP (proves incorruptibility)
  - **Accept**: Auto-lose trial, campaign takes dark turn (PC becomes archdevil in epilogue, Yllmourne "wins" but becomes dystopia)

### The Verdict

**Total Justice Points** (example thresholds):
- **35+ JP**: Overwhelming victory—Yllmourne fully autonomous, infernal claim dismissed
- **25-34 JP**: Victory with conditions—Yllmourne independent but must meet oversight standards
- **15-24 JP**: Split decision—Yllmourne granted probationary independence (5-year review)
- **10-14 JP**: Narrow loss—Infernal Compact gains limited jurisdiction (shared governance)
- **Below 10 JP**: Total loss—Yllmourne annexed by Infernal Compact

**Judge's Pronouncement** (adjust based on JP tier):
> "The Court finds merit in both arguments. Yllmourne's past is stained. Its present, reformed by Talesman [PC name], shows Law's potential. We rule: Yllmourne remains under Maal's jurisdiction, conditional on continued reform. The Infernal Compact's claim is DENIED. However—" [gestures to Sogmonnath issue] "—the existential threat must be resolved. Talesman, you have won your city's soul. Now save its body."

## Original Content: The Seal of Law Ceremony (Grand Finale Part II)

**Level**: 20
**Structure**: Epic ritual encounter combining skill challenges, combat, and cinematic moments

### The Setup

With tribunal won, PC must implement **permanent sealing** of Sogmonnath. This requires:
1. **Donsax** (still embedded in temporary seal at Waterlogged Mountain)
2. **Divine Mandate** (granted by tribunal victory)
3. **Willing Sacrifice** (PC's choice of what to give up)
4. **Cosmic Conjunction** (rare planar alignment, happens *now* or not for century)

**Time Pressure**: Conjunction lasts 4 hours. Ritual requires 3 hours. Infernal Compact, despite losing case, sends saboteurs (they'd rather Sogmonnath destroy evidence than PC succeed).

### Phase 1: Retrieving Donsax

**Challenge**: Remove blade from temporary seal without releasing Sogmonnath

**The Extraction** (Skill Challenge):
- **Round 1** (DC 18 Arcana): Analyze seal structure, identify safe removal sequence
- **Round 2** (DC 20 Religion): Channel divine energy to substitute for Donsax temporarily
- **Round 3** (DC 16 Athletics or Sleight of Hand): Physically extract blade in one smooth motion
- **Failure consequences**: Each failure releases Sogmonnath tendril (CR 12 encounter before continuing)

**Donsax Attunement**:
- Blade recognizes PC as "Law-Bearer"
- Grants temporary legendary item properties:
  - *+3 greatsword*
  - Deals extra 2d8 radiant damage to aberrations, fiends, undead
  - Once per day: cast *holy aura* centered on blade
  - Sentience: Speaks to PC, offers tactical advice (DM voice)

### Phase 2: The Ceremony Site

**Location**: Yllmourne's highest point (church steeple, lighthouse, or cliff overlooking bay)
- Must be visible to entire city (symbolic: citizens witness salvation)
- Must align with celestial conjunction (specific compass bearing, time of day)

**Participants**:
- **PC** (primary ritualist)
- **Minqena & Scholars** (support casters, maintain arcane circle)
- **Rotarist Priest** (channels divine energy)
- **Navula** (represents city's will, holds civic charter)
- **Allied NPCs** (form protective perimeter)

**The Ritual** (3 stages, 1 hour each):

**Stage 1: Invocation of Law** (DC 18 Religion)

**Stage 3: The Seal of Eternity** (DC 22 Religion + Sacrifice)

**Sacrifice Options** (each has narrative consequences):

   - Consequence: Epilogue is bittersweet—PC doesn't recognize friends

3. **Immortality**: Bind PC's soul to Sogmonnath's prison as eternal warden
   - Narrative: "I give my eternity so Yllmourne may have mortal choice."
   - Consequence: PC cannot leave Yllmourne, ages normally but cannot die until Sogmonnath destroyed

4. **Alternative—Shared Sacrifice**: Convince city to bear burden collectively (each citizen gives small piece of vitality)

   - Consequence: Yllmourne's population ages 1 year overnight, but no individual bears full cost
   - Thematic: "Law is not one hero—it's community"

## DM Guidance for Act IV

### Pacing Cosmic-Scale Storytelling

**Challenge**: Epic tier can feel abstract—ground it in personal stakes

**Techniques**:
1. **Constant callbacks**: In celestial courts, reference specific Act I moments ("Remember the mudskipper you saved? Its soul testified.")
2. **Mortal anchors**: Even in planar realms, include Yllmourne citizens as observers/witnesses
3. **Ticking clocks**: Conjunction countdown, Sogmonnath's awakening, trial deadlines create urgency
4. **Personal costs**: Each phase should ask what PC sacrifices (relationships, power, identity)

### Making Divine Litigation Dramatic

**Risk**: Courtroom drama becomes boring exposition

**Solutions**:
1. **Skill checks matter**: Each roll has narrative consequence (failed Persuasion = jury whispers disapprovingly)
2. **Physical stakes**: Trial-by-ordeal breaks up roleplay with combat
3. **NPC witnesses**: Use beloved NPCs as testimonial moments (players care about Minqena's speech)
4. **Adversarial roleplay**: Let player and Naran cross-examine (you play Naran, respond dynamically)

**Example Dynamic**:
- **PC**: "But Cyrullon was defeated!"
- **Naran** (you): "After fifty years. How many died in that half-century? Law is not slow justice—it's injustice."
- **PC must respond**: Forces player to engage philosophically

### Calling Back Early Campaign Moments

Every Act I scene should payoff in Act IV:

- **Mudskipper PC saved**: Its soul appears in celestial court as character witness
- **Dockworker PC helped**: Now union leader, testifies to PC's integrity
- **Corrupt guard PC spared**: Reformed, proves redemption possible
- **Thranengi PC allied with**: Provides warriors for final battle

**Technique**: In session prep, list 10-15 Act I moments and assign each a "payoff" scene in Act IV

### Emotional Payoff Techniques

**Act IV should feel like culmination, not just conclusion**:

1. **Recognition**: NPCs explicitly thank PC ("You saved my daughter's life in Act I—she's ten now, wants to be like you")
2. **Transformation visuals**: Yllmourne changes physically (new buildings, murals of PC, clean streets)
3. **Sacrifice weight**: If PC gives up power/memory/freedom, linger on that loss
4. **Victory lap**: After finale, give 30-min epilogue session where PC walks city, sees impact

### Running Trial-by-Ordeal

**Structure**: Three challenges (Might, Wisdom, Heart) test different character aspects

**DM Principles**:
1. **Might isn't just combat**: Can be athletic challenge, endurance test, or protecting innocents
2. **Wisdom rewards creativity**: No "correct" answer to moral dilemma—assess player's reasoning
3. **Heart is hardest**: Temptation should be genuinely appealing (make infernal offer attractive)

**Adjusting Difficulty**:
- If player struggling, have allied NPC provide hint or assistance
- If player dominating, add complication (celestial champion uses dirty tactic in Might trial, forcing moral choice)

### Sogmonnath Horror

**Maintaining dread**:
- Describe entity in contradictions ("solid liquid," "screaming silence," "ancient newborn")
- Avoid explaining it fully (incomprehensible = scarier)
- Show its power passively (flowers wilt in proximity, time stutters, characters feel existential dread)

**Combat tactics**:
- Don't just hit point grind—use status effects (petrification, aging, item destruction)
- Environmental storytelling (city cracking as entity manifests)
- Make victory feel earned (require specific strategy, not just damage)

### Alternate PC Choices

**If PC chooses dark path**:
- Don't punish—deliver consequences and run epilogue as dark fantasy
- Show PC's "benevolent tyranny" (city prosperous but soulless)
- Offer redemption quest in future campaign (new heroes liberate Yllmourne from fallen PC)

**If PC refuses sacrifice**:
- Sealing can fail (Sogmonnath escapes, Yllmourne evacuated, becomes quarantine zone)
- Bittersweet ending: PC saved selves but lost city
- Emphasize theme: Law requires personal cost

### Session Structure Recommendation

**Act IV spans 8-12 sessions**:
1. **Session 1-2**: Radiance Lost (Foundational Scroll quest)
2. **Session 3-4**: Journey to Courts + Preparation
3. **Session 5-6**: Shadows of Dusk Queen (Architect confrontation)
4. **Session 7-8**: Tribunal (trial phases)
5. **Session 9-10**: Seal of Law Ceremony (final battle)
6. **Session 11**: Epilogue (collaborative worldbuilding, "10 years later" scenes)

## Conclusion

Act IV transforms street-level investigation into cosmic opera while maintaining personal stakes. The PC who saved mudskippers in Act I now argues before solar judges. The city rescued from plague faces existential annihilation. But at its core, the campaign remains about one truth: **Law requires champions, and champions are mortals who choose to act**.

**Final Themes**:
- **Agency over destiny**: PC proved mortals shape their fate, not gods
- **Reform over revolution**: Yllmourne improved through persistent effort, not destruction
- **Cost of justice**: Victory requires sacrifice, but alternative is abdication
- **Legacy**: PC's actions ripple across planes, setting precedent for all cities

**DM's Last Word**:
This campaign is love letter to players who care about NPCs, who track city improvements, who argue philosophy with devils. Reward that investment. Let them see Yllmourne thrive because *they* made it happen. That's the magic—not spells, but agency.

**For the players who saved Yllmourne**: Thank you for bearing witness. May your cities stand forever.
