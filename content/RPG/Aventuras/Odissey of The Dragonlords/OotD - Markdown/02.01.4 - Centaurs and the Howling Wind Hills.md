# The Meeting on the Howling Wind Hills

## Scene 1: The Path to the Council

Following the messenger’s instructions, the heroes travel north for three days toward the Howling Wind Hills. The landscape they cross has visibly transformed. Where once lay a cracked, yellow‑stained steppe, a shy green now grows. The soil is damp, and the air carries the fresh scent of wet earth. The rain—brought by the heroes—has begun to heal the land.

As they approach the hills, the heroes notice they are being watched. Centaur scouts, riding high on distant peaks, shadow their approach. Their vigilance is not hostile, but intensely curious. They are not the fanatical soldiers of Sydon; they are the true children of the steppe, proud and discerning.

The gathering site is an ancient stone circle, a sacred place for the tribes. In the center burns a great bonfire, its smoke spiraling into the cloudy sky. About fifty centaurs are assembled, representing at least three tribes, their rustic banners trembling in the wind. The heroes take note of the intricate designs on their leather armor and saddles—a glimpse of a culture that rarely shares with outsiders. From afar, a melancholic, deep song is silenced abruptly by the arrival of the group.

## Scene 2: The Tribes’ Council

When they arrive, the heroes are led to the center of the circle. The silence is heavy, filled with expectation and wounded pride. Three figures stand out.

- **Baenor, Chief of the Wheat‑Lance** – an imposing mid‑aged centaur with a braided beard and eyes that bear the weight of leadership. He called the gathering. His posture reflects cautious pragmatism, while fierce loyalty to his clan is evident in every tense muscle.

- **Khea, the Flame‑mane Shaman** – an elderly centaur whose coat is almost white. She leans on a twisted staff adorned with feathers and the skulls of small creatures. Her gaze is deep and skeptical, as though she sees the ghosts of broken promises.

- **Rhork, Lord of the Stone‑Helmet War** – a younger, massive centaur covered in scars. He wears bronze plate armor, a clear gift from Sydon. He exudes arrogance and raw power, looking at the heroes with disguised contempt.

**Baenor** steps forward, his resonant, deep voice echoing across the circle.

> **Baenor:**  
> “Unbreakables. Prophecy Heroes. You have come. Look around you. See this green? Feel this dampness? That is your deed. Sydon promised us much. He sang his song to us: _‘Seven tribes, I gave three blades, and silver crowns to another seven.’_ But those silver crowns have become collars, and the blades serve only his interests. For a season he gave us power in exchange for loyalty, but he left us thirsty. You challenged the Rock, and the heavens wept with relief. That made us think. Loyalty to a Titan who leaves us dying of thirst is perhaps folly.”

Before the heroes can answer, **Rhork** slams his foot down, a sound like thunder.

> **Rhork:**  
> “Folly? Folly is to listen to wanderers who are merely lucky! Sydon gave us **iron hearts**! Strength to crush our enemies! This rain is a trick. You come here with your soft hands from villages, speaking of hope? Hope does not fill a belly or sharpen a blade. Power does! The Master of All will make us masters of Thylea, and in the end, *‘All shall bow before me!’* And that includes you.”

He spits on the ground toward the heroes. Tension rises, and several centaurs of the Stone‑Helmet tribe grip their weapons tighter, their martial skill evident in their stance.

It is now the old **Khea** who speaks, her voice a hoarse hiss slicing through the wind.

> **Khea:**  
> “The War Lord talks of power, but forgets its price. Sydon gave us jewels, yes. But he took ours. *‘To each tribe, I gave white jewels, as stars over the wine‑colored sea…’* but where are our songs? Where is our art? We traded the stories of our ancestors for hymns to a tyrant. Our children march in wars that are not ours. And you… you are different? Or are you just different masters, with different promises? The gods abandoned us once. The Dragon Lords hunted us. Why should we believe that your path is the true one?”

She locks eyes on the heroes, scrutinizing their souls. She does not seek power, but truth.

## Scene 3: The Offer and the Division

**Baenor** raises a hand, silencing the murmurs. He turns to the heroes with a serious, direct gaze.

> **Baenor:**  
> “Words are not enough. The tribes are divided. Rhork and his followers have drunk the power of Sydon. Khea and the elders fear a new betrayal. I… I seek a future for our people. Therefore, I make you an offer. A test.”  
> 
> “To the east, in the ruins of an ancient titan fort, one of Sydon’s generals has set up a forward post. A brutal cyclops named **Geryon, the Shredder**. He commands a legion of basilisk and harpy, and has enslaved patrols of centaurs who refused to use Sydon’s silver chains. He is a thorn in our flank and a symbol of the Titan's power in our lands.”  
> 
> **The Proposal:** “Go there. Destroy the forward post. Kill Geryon and free our brothers. Show us that your power is not merely luck, but a force capable of breaking the Titans' chains. Do this, and the Wheat‑Lance tribe and all who follow me will ride beside you when war comes. You will have allies in the steppe.”  
> 
> **The Condition:** “But if you fail… or refuse… then know that you will find no friends here. And the next time we meet, it will be from the opposite side of the battlefield.”

Rhork lets out a cruel laugh. “A task for heroes! Sending them to death, Baenor? Clever. They will be a fine feast for Geryon’s basilisk.”

The council concludes. The decision lies in the hands of the heroes.

### Consequences and Next Steps

The heroes now have a clear mission with direct, significant consequences:

- **The Uncertain Alliance** – Accepting Baenor’s proposal is the sole way to gain the centaurs as allies. He may offer a guide to lead them to Geryon’s territory and information on the defenses of the forward post.

- **A New Enemy** – Rhork and the Stone‑Helmet tribe are now declared antagonists. They may try to ambush the heroes on the way to Geryon’s fort or send a warning to the cyclops about their arrival.

- **The Heart of the Steppes** – Khea remains neutral, but watchful. If the heroes demonstrate respect for her traditions or the land, she may provide secret aid—a protection amulet against the petrifying gaze of the basilisk or a blessing to calm the winds. She embodies the “soul” of the centaur people the heroes must also win.

The heroes’ fame from the Rock and the rain’s relevance now set the stage: a mission to defeat Geryon that could bring an army—or, conversely, a burial ground—into the steppe.
