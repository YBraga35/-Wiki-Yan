# Special Mechanics Guide

This comprehensive mechanics reference covers custom systems for running **Tales from the Shadows** as an epic solo campaign (levels 1-20) in the Yllmourne setting. These mechanics support investigation-focused gameplay, faction dynamics, solo combat balance, and divine progression.

---

## Section 1: Faction Clocks System

### Overview

Faction clocks track the escalating crises facing Yllmourne using 0-6 segment clocks. These clocks advance based on PC actions (or inaction) and create dynamic tension throughout the campaign. Unlike traditional timers, these clocks don't advance automatically—they respond to the narrative.

**How Clocks Work**:
- Each clock starts at 0 segments filled
- Clocks advance when specific triggers occur
- Multiple clocks can advance simultaneously
- Clocks can be reset or reduced through PC intervention
- Communicate clock status through NPC dialogue, rumors, and environmental descriptions

### The Five Clocks

#### 1. Mudskipper Plague Clock

**What It Represents**: The spreading supernatural plague devastating Yllmourne's port district.

**Segments**:
- **0 - Calm**: Isolated cases, easily contained. Hospitals managing normally.
- **1 - Early Spread**: Multiple cases daily. Healers concerned but not overwhelmed.
- **2 - Acceleration**: Docks quarantined. First deaths among wealthy families.
- **3 - Epidemic**: Hospitals full. Bodies burning in streets. Panic buying.
- **4 - Civil Strain**: Quarantine enforced by guards. Food shortages. Riots.
- **5 - Collapse Imminent**: Mass graves. City services failing. Exodus attempts.
- **6 - Total Collapse**: Apocalyptic death toll. Government breakdown. Yllmourne abandoned.

**Advancement Triggers**:
- PC fails plague-related Investigation or Medicine check by 5+
- PC ignores plague symptoms in NPCs for 2+ sessions
- PC disrupts medical supplies or healers
- Thranengi ritual site remains undiscovered for 4+ sessions (Act I)
- Cure components destroyed or contaminated
- Dagmara's research falls into wrong hands

**Reset/Reduction Conditions**:
- Discover plague source: reduce by 1
- Distribute partial cure: reduce by 2
- Complete Thranengi ritual/destroy artifact: reduce to 0
- Perform mass healing ceremony with Maal's blessing: reduce by 1-2

**Consequences by Segment**:

| Segment | Mechanical Effects | Narrative Effects |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1-2 | Healing supplies cost 2x normal. Medicine checks DC +2. | Rumors spread. Docks avoided. |
| 3-4 | Healing supplies cost 5x. Long rests in city don't remove exhaustion. | Quarantine restricts movement. Guards suspicious. |
| 5-6 | Healing supplies unavailable. Constitution saves vs. infection (DC 15) daily. | Mass death. NPCs flee or die. Faction leadership changes. |

**Using in Play**: Telegraph the clock's advancement through increasingly dire descriptions. At segment 0, dock workers cough. At segment 3, the PC steps over corpses. At segment 5, describe empty streets and burning pyres.

#### 2. Cy vs. Sefton War Clock

**What It Represents**: The escalating conflict between Yllmourne's two major crime families.

**Segments**:
- **0 - Uneasy Truce**: Cold war. Territories respected. Minimal violence.
- **1 - Proxy Conflicts**: Street gangs clash. Sabotage attempts.
- **2 - Assassinations Begin**: Key lieutenants targeted. Reprisal killings.
- **3 - Open Warfare**: Turf battles in streets. Civilian casualties.
- **4 - Total War**: Full mobilization. Fire bombings. District sieges.
- **5 - Pyrrhic Victory**: One family wins but city in ruins.
- **6 - Mutual Destruction**: Both families destroyed. Power vacuum chaos.

**Advancement Triggers**:
- PC exposes criminal operations without offering escape valve
- PC kills/arrests lieutenant of either family
- PC sides obviously with one family against the other
- Maritime resources disrupted (links to Clock #5)
- Library threatened (both families use their legal services)
- PC refuses mediation attempts

**Reset/Reduction Conditions**:
- Broker peace treaty: reduce by 2
- Redirect both families toward common enemy (plague, Church): reduce by 1
- Remove extremist leaders from both sides: reduce by 1
- Expose third party manipulating the conflict: reduce to 0

**Consequences by Segment**:

| Segment | Mechanical Effects | Narrative Effects |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1-2 | Random street encounters (thugs CR 1/2) when traveling. Bribery costs increase. | Tension visible. Guards on edge. |
| 3-4 | Street battles daily (CR 3-5 encounters). Shops close. Disadvantage on Persuasion with frightened civilians. | Bodies in streets. Districts locked down. |
| 5-6 | Building fires (Dex saves DC 15 to avoid debris). NPCs flee city. Faction leaders unavailable. | Warzones. Entire blocks destroyed. Children orphaned. |

**Using in Play**: Show the conflict through NPC interactions. At segment 0, enforcers glare at each other. At segment 3, the PC interrupts a firefight. Let the PC choose whether to intervene or leverage the chaos.

#### 3. Library Inquisition Clock

**What It Represents**: The Church of Gliriel's growing persecution of the Library of Lore.

**Segments**:
- **0 - Suspicion**: Sermons warning against "dangerous knowledge."
- **1 - Surveillance**: Church agents monitor library visitors.
- **2 - Harassment**: Intimidation of patrons. "Random" inspections.
- **3 - Legal Action**: Confiscation threats. Arrests of librarians.
- **4 - Purge Begins**: Book burnings. Library occupation.
- **5 - Total Suppression**: Library closed. Staff imprisoned or executed.
- **6 - Theocracy**: Church controls all information. Inquisition established.

**Advancement Triggers**:
- PC openly defies Church authority
- PC discovered researching forbidden topics (planar lore, Sogmonnath)
- Faylene's hex worsens (links to Clock #4)
- PC fails to defend library from accusations
- Dagmara's research revealed to contain heretical material
- PC assists known heretics or apostates

**Reset/Reduction Conditions**:
- Break Faylene's hex: reduce to 0
- Expose Church corruption publicly: reduce by 2
- Secure legal protections for library: reduce by 1
- Remove extremist priests from positions of power: reduce by 1
- Prove library knowledge essential to plague cure: reduce by 2

**Consequences by Segment**:

| Segment | Mechanical Effects | Narrative Effects |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1-2 | Disadvantage on Persuasion with Church NPCs. Research takes 2x time. | Whispered conversations. Patrons afraid. |
| 3-4 | Access to library forbidden without church escort. Certain books removed/destroyed. | Book burnings in square. Arrests. Fear. |
| 5-6 | Library inaccessible. Knowledge checks DC +5. Church NPCs hostile on sight. | Cultural destruction. Underground resistance forms. |

**Using in Play**: The library is a crucial resource for investigation. Let the clock create urgency—can the PC find answers before access is cut off? Use sympathetic librarian NPCs to personalize the stakes.

#### 4. Hex on Faylene Clock

**What It Represents**: The supernatural corruption of High Priestess Faylene, causing her descent into tyranny.

**Segments**:
- **0 - Doubt**: Faylene experiences troubling visions, keeps them private.
- **1 - Paranoia**: She sees threats everywhere. Inner circle notices changes.
- **2 - Hardline Shift**: Harsh sermons. Zero tolerance policies.
- **3 - Persecution**: Witch hunts begin. Accusations of heresy increase.
- **4 - Tyranny**: Summary executions. Inquisitorial courts. Fear reign.
- **5 - Madness**: Faylene completely controlled. Apocalyptic prophecies.
- **6 - Divine War**: Faylene declares crusade. Mass violence in Gliriel's name.

**Advancement Triggers**:
- PC confronts Faylene without evidence of hex
- PC desecrates Church property
- Plague spreads (Church sees as divine punishment)
- Library persecuted successfully (reinforces hex's narrative)
- PC discovers hex but fails to break it
- Time passes without investigation (1 segment per 3 sessions)

**Reset/Reduction Conditions**:
- Identify hex source: reduce by 1
- Discover hexer's identity: reduce by 1
- Perform counter-ritual: reduce by 2
- Destroy hex focus object: reduce to 0
- Kill/neutralize hexer: reduce to 0
- Convince Faylene's inner circle to intervene: reduce by 1

**Consequences by Segment**:

| Segment | Mechanical Effects | Narrative Effects |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1-2 | Healing services cost 3x. Church NPCs suspicious (Insight DC 12 reveals their unease). | Sermons grow darker. Loyal priests concerned. |
| 3-4 | Church refuses services to "suspicious" individuals. Witch trials (Persuasion DC 18 to defend accused). | Public executions. Informant culture. Families torn apart. |
| 5-6 | Active persecution of PC (CR-appropriate bounty hunters). Divine magic in city suppressed (spells require Arcana DC 15 check). | Theocratic police state. Citizens flee or convert. |

**Using in Play**: Faylene should initially appear as a sympathetic figure struggling against inner darkness. Let the PC discover the hex organically through investigation. Her descent mirrors the city's corruption.

#### 5. Maritime Blockade Clock

**What It Represents**: The strangling of Yllmourne's sea trade, causing economic collapse.

**Segments**:
- **0 - Free Trade**: Normal maritime commerce. Tariffs standard.
- **1 - Toll Increases**: Harbor fees triple. "Protection" rackets.
- **2 - Selective Harassment**: Rival ships turned away. Inspections delay cargo.
- **3 - Partial Blockade**: Only "approved" vessels allowed. Smuggling increases.
- **4 - Strict Blockade**: Naval patrols prevent most shipping. Food scarce.
- **5 - Siege**: Nothing in or out. Starvation begins. Desperation.
- **6 - Famine**: Mass starvation. Cannibalism. Social breakdown.

**Advancement Triggers**:
- PC disrupts legitimate shipping operations
- Crime families lose control of docks (links to Clock #2)
- Plague prevents ships from docking (links to Clock #1)
- Thranengi attacks increase (creature active, not contained)
- PC antagonizes harbor master or naval authority
- Foreign powers take advantage of city's weakness

**Reset/Reduction Conditions**:
- Negotiate trade agreement with blockaders: reduce by 2
- Destroy/drive off Thranengi: reduce by 1
- Cure plague (ships willing to dock again): reduce by 1
- Establish smuggling network: reduce by 1 (temporary fix)
- Defeat naval blockade force: reduce to 0 (Act III climactic battle)
- Broker alliance between crime families and merchants: reduce by 1

**Consequences by Segment**:

| Segment | Mechanical Effects | Narrative Effects |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 1-2 | Equipment/supplies cost 2x. Ship travel requires permits (bribes). | Grumbling merchants. Increased prices. |
| 3-4 | Food costs 5x. Constitution saves vs. exhaustion (DC 12) every 3 days. Ship travel nearly impossible. | Bread riots. Children begging. Malnutrition visible. |
| 5-6 | Starvation mechanics (Constitution saves DC 15 daily). No supplies available for purchase. NPCs turn violent for food. | Apocalyptic famine. Bodies in streets. Moral collapse. |

**Using in Play**: The blockade is an economic and moral crisis. Force the PC to make hard choices—save individuals or address systemic problem? Use hungry children and desperate families to personalize the stakes.

### Interplay Between Clocks

Clocks don't exist in isolation. Use these connections to create cascading crises:

- **Plague → Blockade**: Ships refuse to dock at plague-ridden port (Clock 1 at 3+ advances Clock 5)
- **Plague → Church**: Faylene interprets plague as divine punishment, accelerating hex influence (Clock 1 at 2+ advances Clock 4)
- **War → Blockade**: Crime families control docks; their war disrupts shipping (Clock 2 at 3+ advances Clock 5)
- **Church → Library**: As Faylene grows tyrannical, library persecution intensifies (Clock 4 at 2+ advances Clock 3)
- **Church → War**: If Church cracks down on both crime families, they unite against common enemy (Clock 4 at 3+ reduces Clock 2)

### Visual Tracking Methods

**For DM**:
- Keep clock chart in session notes
- Update after significant PC actions or failures
- Review clocks during session prep

**For Player**:
- Describe conditions narratively rather than mechanically
- Offer Investigation/Insight checks (DC 12-15) to assess situation
- Use NPC dialogue: "The plague's getting worse" vs. "Half the dock district is dead"
- Show, don't tell: Describe increasing numbers of corpses, violent incidents, burned books

**Optional**: If your player enjoys meta-knowledge, show actual clock diagrams. Some players engage more deeply when they see the mechanical stakes.

## Section 2: Investigation Mechanics

### The Three-Clue Rule

**Core Principle**: For every crucial piece of information the PC needs to advance the story, provide at least three different ways to discover it.

**What Counts as "Crucial"**:
- Identity of a murderer (Act I: Dagmara's killer)
- Location of quest objective (Act I: Thranengi lair)
- Method to solve central problem (Act II: how to break Faylene's hex)
- Motivation of major antagonist (Act III: why Sogmonnath was imprisoned)

**What Doesn't Count as "Crucial"**:
- Background lore enrichment
- Shortcuts to solutions
- Bonus rewards or hidden treasures
- Optional side quest leads

**Implementation Example - Discovering Dagmara's Murderer**:

Three clue paths:
1. **Autopsy Evidence** (Investigation): Examine body, discover unique poison used only by certain guild
2. **Witness Testimony** (Persuasion/Intimidation): Interview dock workers who saw suspicious figure
3. **Financial Records** (Investigation): Library records show someone paid for specific alchemical components

Each path leads to same conclusion but rewards different PC approaches.

**Creating Redundant Clue Paths**:
1. List the crucial revelation
2. Identify 5-7 potential evidence sources (people, places, documents)
3. Assign clue strength (strong/weak) to each
4. Distribute clues across different skill sets (Investigation, Persuasion, Arcana, Medicine, etc.)
5. Ensure at least 3 strong clues are accessible early

### Clue Types

#### Strong Clues
**Definition**: Direct evidence that unambiguously points to conclusion.

**Examples**:
- Autopsy report identifying poison
- Eyewitness testimony naming suspect
- Physical evidence (murder weapon with fingerprints)
- Written confession or incriminating letter
- Magical scrying revealing truth

**Acquisition**: Require successful skill checks (DC 12-15) or social interaction. Not hidden behind obscure solutions.

**Presentation**: "You find a letter in coded script. After deciphering (Investigation DC 14), it clearly states..."

#### Weak Clues
**Definition**: Circumstantial evidence that suggests but doesn't prove.

**Examples**:
- Rumors about suspect's whereabouts
- Suspicious behavior patterns
- Financial motive
- Opportunity to commit crime
- Inconsistent alibis

**Acquisition**: Easier to obtain (DC 10-12) or given freely through observation.

**Presentation**: "The shopkeeper avoids eye contact when mentioning the night of the murder. Insight DC 12 reveals he's hiding something."

**Using Weak Clues**: Three weak clues pointing the same direction = strong suspicion. PC must still gather strong evidence for definitive conclusion.

### Distribution Guidelines

For each major mystery, plant:
- **3 strong clues** (definitive proof)
- **3 weak clues** (circumstantial evidence)
- **2 red herrings** (false leads that require investigation to disprove)
- **1 breakthrough clue** (hidden behind difficult DC 18+ check, provides major revelation)

### Investigation Structure

#### Phase 1: Opening Scene
**Purpose**: Present the mystery. Establish stakes. Provide initial clues.

**Structure**:
- Crime scene or incident
- NPC witness/victim provides context
- 1-2 obvious clues available immediately
- 3-4 investigation opportunities identified

**Example - Dagmara's Murder**:
- Scene: Body discovered in library archive
- Initial clues: Signs of struggle, toxic residue on lips, missing research notes
- Investigation opportunities: Interview librarians, examine body, search her chambers, review recent visitors

**DM Prep**:
- Map location with numbered clue markers
- Prepare 3-5 NPC reactions
- List obvious and hidden clues (with DCs)

#### Phase 2: Gathering Phase
**Purpose**: PC collects evidence through investigation, interviewing, research.

**Structure**:
- **Skill Challenges**: Investigation, Perception, Medicine, Insight
- **Social Encounters**: Interview witnesses, interrogate suspects
- **Research**: Library archives, legal records, historical documents
- **Exploration**: Search crime scenes, suspect locations, related sites

**Time Management**:
- Each investigative action = 1-4 hours game time
- Track time passage (clocks may advance if PC dawdles)
- Provide time estimates: "Thoroughly searching this warehouse will take 2 hours"

**DM Tools**:
- Clue tracking sheet (check off as PC discovers)
- NPC interview notes (what they know, what they'll reveal)
- Location descriptions with hidden clue DCs

#### Phase 3: Analysis Phase
**Purpose**: PC connects dots, forms theories, plans confrontation.

**Structure**:
- Review evidence collected
- NPC ally helps synthesize (sidekick's role)
- Identify gaps in knowledge
- Form testable hypothesis

**Mechanics**:
- Allow PC to make Intelligence (Investigation) check to connect disparate clues
- DC 12: Notice basic patterns
- DC 15: Form coherent theory
- DC 18: Identify hidden implications

**DM Role**:
- Don't require this check to proceed—smart player analysis should suffice
- Use check to provide hints if player is stuck
- Offer partial success: "You're certain about X, but Y doesn't fit the pattern yet"

#### Phase 4: Confrontation Phase
**Purpose**: Test theory against reality. Reveal truth. Resolve mystery.

**Structure**:
- Confront suspect/visit crucial location
- Theory tested (was PC correct?)
- Final revelation (who, why, how)
- Choice point (how to resolve?)

**Complications**:
- Suspect flees (chase scene)
- Suspect fights (combat encounter)
- Suspect confesses but begs for mercy (moral dilemma)
- Evidence destroyed (must rely on testimony)
- Wrong theory (red herring revealed, must reassess)

**DM Prep**:
- Multiple confrontation outcomes prepared
- Stat blocks ready if combat occurs
- Moral dilemma framed (justice vs. mercy, law vs. truth)

### Skill Challenges for Investigation

**Multi-Skill Investigation Format**:

**Setup**: Complex investigation requires multiple successful checks.

**Structure**:
- Define goal (e.g., "Determine who poisoned Dagmara")
- Set success threshold (e.g., 6 successes before 3 failures)
- List relevant skills with DCs and outcomes

**Example - Crime Scene Analysis**:

| Skill | DC | Success | Failure |
|-------|-----|---------|---------|
| Investigation | 14 | Discover hidden evidence (+1 success) | Contaminate scene (disadvantage on next check) |
| Medicine | 13 | Identify cause of death (+1 success) | Misdiagnose, waste time (-1 hour) |
| Perception | 12 | Notice suspicious detail (+1 success) | Miss obvious clue (DM provides hint) |
| Arcana | 15 | Detect magical residue (+1 success) | No magical component detected (red herring?) |
| Insight | 14 | Read witness body language (+1 success) | Misread nervousness as guilt (false lead) |

**Partial Success Mechanics**:
- **4-5 successes**: Learn most information, miss minor details
- **6+ successes**: Complete picture, bonus clue revealed
- **3 failures**: Learn enough to proceed but with complications (rumor spreads, suspect alerted, time lost)

**Time Pressure**: Each check = 30 minutes to 1 hour. Clock advancement possible if investigation takes multiple sessions.

### Evidence Tracking

#### For the DM

**Clue Database**:
Create spreadsheet or note with:
- Clue description
- Source (where/who provides it)
- Revelation (what it proves)
- Strength (strong/weak)
- Status (discovered/undiscovered)

**Session Prep**:
- Review what PC has discovered
- Prepare reminders of past clues
- Identify remaining paths to truth

#### For the Player

**In-Character Journal**:
Encourage player to keep investigation notes:
- NPC names and testimonies
- Physical evidence descriptions
- Theories and suspicions
- Questions to answer

**DM Support**:
- Provide written handouts for crucial evidence
- Offer Investigation check to recall past clues
- NPC sidekick can summarize: "So far we know..."

**Visual Aids**:
- Maps of crime scenes
- NPC relationship diagrams
- Timeline of events
- Evidence photos (if using digital tools)

**Memory Checks**:
If player forgets crucial information from previous session:
- Intelligence (Investigation) DC 10 to recall
- Automatic success if taking notes
- NPC can remind: "Don't you remember when we found..."

### Red Herrings

**Purpose**: Create false leads that add complexity without frustrating.

**Good Red Herrings**:
- Require investigation to disprove
- Lead to interesting side content
- Make sense within world logic
- Don't waste excessive time

**Example - The Suspicious Sailor**:
- Clues suggest sailor had access to poison and motive
- Investigation reveals he's guilty of unrelated smuggling, not murder
- Disproves theory but creates side quest option (leverage his smuggling, report him, or ignore)

**Bad Red Herrings**:
- Arbitrary misdirection with no logical basis
- Require exhaustive investigation with no payoff
- Frustrate player without adding value

**Implementation**: 1-2 red herrings per major mystery. Ensure disproving them reveals useful information (worldbuilding, side opportunities, character development).

## Section 3: Shadow Corruption System

### What is Shadow Corruption?

In Yllmourne, "shadow corruption" represents the bleeding of extraplanar influence into the material world. Unlike traditional Shadowfell mechanics, this corruption stems from:
- **Naran's Grasp**: The chaos god's agents spreading discord
- **Thellos's Judgement**: The balance god's cold assessment warping morality
- **Sogmonnath's Stirring**: The imprisoned leviathan's psychic emanations
- **Cursed Locations**: Sites where planar barriers weaken

**Narrative Function**: Shadow corruption represents the moral and spiritual cost of investigating cosmic conspiracies. It's a measure of how deeply the PC peers into the abyss.

### Corruption Exposure

**When PC Gains Corruption**:
- Entering shadow pockets (fog-shrouded ruins, cursed sites, planar thin spots)
- Prolonged contact with cursed NPCs (Faylene under hex, Sogmonnath cultists)
- Using dark magic items or forbidden rituals
- Witnessing or participating in acts of cosmic horror
- Failing saves against corruption effects
- Investigating planar conspiracy too deeply without spiritual protection

**Exposure by Tier**:

**Levels 1-7 (Minor Exposure)**:
- Urban investigation, mundane corruption
- Exposure: 1 corruption point per significant event
- Sources: Cursed murder scenes, plague victims, minor cultists

**Levels 8-12 (Moderate Exposure)**:
- Deeper conspiracy, supernatural threats
- Exposure: 1-2 corruption points per session in corrupted areas
- Sources: Sogmonnath's prison, planar rifts, powerful curses

**Levels 13-20 (Major Exposure)**:
- Extraplanar courts, cosmic entities
- Exposure: 2-3 corruption points per significant encounter
- Sources: Infernal lawyers, fey tribunals, divine trials

### Corruption Mechanics

**Corruption Point Scale**: Track from 0-10

**Gaining Corruption**:
- Failed Wisdom or Charisma save (DC = 10 + corruption source CR/level): gain 1 point
- Critical failure: gain 2 points
- Voluntary exposure (using cursed item, performing dark ritual): gain points as specified
- Automatic gain: Certain story events inflict unavoidable corruption

**Effects by Corruption Level**:

#### 1-3 Points (Minor Corruption)
**Narrative Effects**:
- Disturbing dreams (visions of Sogmonnath, drowning sensations)
- Growing unease in sunlight or open spaces
- Whispers at edge of hearing
- Shadows seem to move wrong

**Mechanical Effects**:
- None (narrative flavor only)
- **Optional Rule**: Disadvantage on saving throws vs. fear from corrupted sources

**Roleplaying**: Describe physical sensations. PC feels watched. Brief paranoia.

#### 4-6 Points (Moderate Corruption)
**Narrative Effects**:
- Visible signs: shadows cling to PC, eyes reflect strangely, voice echoes
- NPCs notice "something wrong" (Insight DC 10)
- Animals avoid PC
- Disturbing dreams intensify (wake unrefreshed)

**Mechanical Effects**:
- Disadvantage on Persuasion checks with holy NPCs (clerics, paladins, devout civilians)
- Advantage on Intimidation checks against fearful targets
- Corruption whispers grant advantage on Insight checks vs. corrupted NPCs
- **Curse-like Effect**: Once per day, DM can impose disadvantage on a saving throw

**Roleplaying**: PC struggles with intrusive thoughts. Temptation to embrace corruption.

#### 7-9 Points (Major Corruption)
**Narrative Effects**:
- Obvious corruption: PC's shadow moves independently, fog follows them, whispers audible to others
- NPCs react with fear or hostility (Persuasion disadvantage except with corrupted)
- Divine magic causes discomfort (healing hurts, holy symbols burn)
- Dreams are nightmares every night (exhaustion risk)

**Mechanical Effects**:
- Disadvantage on all Charisma checks with non-corrupted NPCs
- Advantage on all Charisma checks with corrupted NPCs
- Resistance to necrotic damage
- Vulnerability to radiant damage
- Once per long rest, DM can force PC to make Wisdom save (DC 15) or take action influenced by corruption (attack ally, reveal secret, perform dark act)

**Roleplaying**: PC's personality shifts. Moral compass wavers. Identity crisis.

#### 10 Points (Critical Corruption)
**Narrative Effects**:
- PC transformed: eyes turn black, shadow becomes tangible, aura of wrongness
- Most NPCs flee on sight
- Holy locations cause pain (Constitution save DC 15 or take 2d6 radiant damage per round)
- Dreams merge with reality (hallucinations)

**Mechanical Effects**:
- **Permanent Consequence** (choose one):
  1. **Shadowed Soul**: PC becomes partially incorporeal (resistance to non-magical damage, but vulnerability to radiant and force)
  2. **Cursed Existence**: PC gains dark gift (shadow step 30 ft. as bonus action 3/day) but loses ability to benefit from divine magic
  3. **Planar Marked**: PC attracts extraplanar attention (random encounters with celestials/fiends investigating the "anomaly")
  4. **Corruption Internalized**: Reduce to 8 corruption, but PC permanently changes (alignment shift, personality trait inverted, physical mutation)

**DM Note**: Reaching 10 corruption should be rare and significant. It's a story climax, not a punishment.

### Reducing Corruption

**Methods to Remove Corruption**:

**Maal's Blessings** (see Section 5):
- Divine favor cleanses: reduce by 1 per blessing tier increase
- Spending blessing use: sacrifice daily blessing use to reduce by 1

**Sacred Rituals**:
- **Lesser Restoration** (cast at sacred site): DC 15 Religion check, reduce by 1 (1/week)
- **Greater Restoration**: automatically reduce by 2
- **Purification Ceremony**: 8-hour ritual, Religion check DC = 10 + corruption points, success reduces by 1d4

**Destroying Corruption Sources**:
- Defeat corrupted entity causing exposure: reduce by 1-3
- Seal planar rift: reduce by 2
- Break curse on NPC: reduce by 1
- Destroy artifact: reduce by 2-4

**Long Rest in Sanctified Locations**:
- Maal's temple: reduce by 1 per week of rest
- Gliriel's shrine (before corruption): reduce by 1 per 3 days
- Natural sacred groves: reduce by 1 per 2 weeks

**Story Milestones**:
- Completing major arc of redemption: reduce by 2-4
- Performing act of heroic sacrifice: reduce by 1d6
- Receiving divine absolution: reduce to 0 (epic moment, level 15+)

**Cannot Reduce Below**:
- Some story events inflict permanent corruption (1-2 points)
- Represents scars that never fully heal
- These points don't cause mechanical effects but remain as narrative reminder

### Optional Rule: Narrative-Only Corruption

**Alternative Approach**: If mechanical penalties disrupt investigation flow, use corruption purely as narrative tool.

**Implementation**:
- Track corruption points as written
- Describe increasing corruption effects (dreams, appearance, NPC reactions)
- Don't apply mechanical disadvantages
- Corruption becomes roleplaying opportunity rather than penalty
- Reduces corruption at same rate but removes pressure

**When to Use**: For players who prefer investigation focus without resource management pressure. Or if solo play makes penalties too punishing.

## Section 4: Solo Combat Balance

### Challenge Adjustments for 1 PC

**Core Principle**: Traditional D&D balances around 4-6 PCs. Solo play requires fundamental redesign.

#### Enemy Numbers Reduction

**Standard Encounter Adjustments**:
- **Deadly Encounter** (DMG standards): Reduce creature count by 75%
- **Hard Encounter**: Reduce by 66%
- **Medium Encounter**: Reduce by 50%
- **Easy Encounter**: Reduce by 33% or convert to roleplaying scene

**Single Powerful Foes**:
Instead of swarms, use:
- Elite versions of standard creatures (+50% HP, +2 to hit, +1 AC)
- Solo monsters with legendary actions (use existing templates or add 2-3 legendary actions)
- Environmental advantages (boss has cover, terrain hazards, escape routes)

**Example - Dock Thugs**:
- **Standard**: 6 thugs (CR 1/2 each) = Hard encounter for 4 PCs
- **Solo Adjusted**: 1 thug captain (use **Bandit Captain** stat block, CR 2) + environmental hazards (falling crates, slippery dock)

**Action Economy Balance**

**The Problem**: Solo PC gets 1 action/turn. Multiple enemies overwhelm through sheer volume of attacks.

**Solutions**:

1. **DMPC Sidekick (Primary Solution)**

**Scribe-Jurist Sidekick** (Expert archetype from Tasha's Cauldron of Everything):

**Stats** (adjust by PC level):
- **Levels 1-4**: Use basic Expert sidekick, emphasize Help action
- **Levels 5-10**: Gain expertise in Investigation, Persuasion, Insight
- **Levels 11+**: Add spellcasting (Bard list, support/utility only)

**Combat Role**:
- **Primary**: Help action (grant advantage to PC)
- **Secondary**: Positioning (flanking, blocking escape routes)
- **Tertiary**: Support spells (healing word, bless, guidance)
- **Never**: Deal primary damage or overshadow PC's combat prowess

**Narrative Role**:
- Legal expertise (interpret contracts, know procedures)
- Social doors (connections PC lacks)
- Evidence recording (takes notes, draws diagrams)
- Voice of reason (offers alternatives, asks questions)

**Skills**:
- Investigation +7 (expertise)
- Persuasion +6 (expertise)
- Insight +5
- Thieves' tools (for evidence gathering, not theft)
- Scribe's tools (document analysis)

**Personality** (DM's choice, but suggestions):
- Idealistic but practical
- Believes in law but questions unjust laws
- Admires PC's dedication to justice
- Has personal stake (family affected by plague, friend wrongly accused, etc.)

**IMPORTANT BOUNDARIES**:
- Sidekick never solves mysteries for PC
- Sidekick offers options, not solutions: "We could check the harbor records, or interview the night watchman, or..."
- Sidekick can be wrong (make Intelligence checks, fail sometimes)
- Sidekick has own moral code (may disagree with PC's choices, creating tension)
- Sidekick is mortal (can be threatened, wounded, corrupted—creates stakes)

**Sidekick in Combat**:
- Initiative: Acts on PC's turn (same initiative count)
- Action economy: PC directs as bonus action ("Help me" or "Cast healing word")
- **Alternative**: Sidekick acts independently but always supports (DM-controlled but predictable)

2. **Legendary Actions for Solo PC**

**Optional Rule**: Grant PC legendary-style actions to balance action economy.

**Heroic Actions** (3 per round, recharge at start of PC's turn):
- **Cost 1 Action**: Move up to speed, make opportunity attack, use object
- **Cost 2 Actions**: Make single weapon attack, cast cantrip, use class feature (1st level or lower)
- **Cost 3 Actions**: Take full action (Attack action with multiple attacks, cast leveled spell)

**Balance**: Powerful but doesn't break game. Enemies still threaten but PC can respond dynamically.

**When to Use**: If not using sidekick, or for epic boss fights (levels 15+) where PC should feel legendary.

3. **Reduce Enemy Attack Frequency**

**Elite Enemy Design**:
- Fewer, stronger enemies
- Each enemy gets 1 attack/turn, but hits harder (+2 damage die size)
- Focus on interesting abilities over quantity of attacks

**Example**: Instead of 4 cultists making 4 attacks/round, 1 cult fanatic makes 1 attack but deals 2d8+3 instead of 1d6+1.

### Threat Clocks Instead of Combat

**Alternative Resolution**: Some encounters become clock-based challenges rather than HP attrition.

**When to Use**:
- Large-scale battles (ship combat, sieges)
- Chase scenes
- Timed rescues
- Environmental disasters

**Structure**:
- Create 4-6 segment clock representing goal
- PC makes skill checks or attacks to fill segments
- Enemies/environment make "attacks" that:
  - Deal damage to PC
  - Deplete resources
  - Create complications
- Fill clock before PC defeated = victory

**Example: The Thranengi Hunt**

**Setup**: Sea monster (Thranengi) attacks ship. Traditional combat = TPK risk for solo PC.

**Clock Alternative**:
- **Goal**: Drive off Thranengi (6-segment clock)
- **Victory**: Fill all 6 segments
- **Defeat**: Ship takes 100 HP damage (treat ship as separate entity)

**PC Actions** (each round):
- **Harpoon Attack**: Ranged attack vs. AC 15, hit = fill 1 segment
- **Critical Hit**: Fill 2 segments
- **Divine Smite/Spell (2nd level+)**: Fill 2 segments
- **Rally Crew**: Persuasion DC 14, success = crew fills 1 segment next round

**Thranengi Responses** (each round):
- **Tentacle Slam**: Ship takes 2d10 damage
- **Drag Below**: PC makes Strength save DC 14 or dragged overboard (disadvantage on attacks until rescued)
- **Thrashing**: All on deck make Dexterity save DC 12 or knocked prone

**Outcome**:
- **6 segments filled**: Thranengi retreats, wounded. PC gains trophy (Thranengi hide for crafting).
- **Ship destroyed first**: Shipwreck survival challenge. PC survives but loses cargo/NPCs.

**Partial Success**: Fill 4-5 segments before ship critical = Thranengi flees but ship badly damaged (expensive repairs).

### Partial Failure Mechanics

**Philosophy**: Solo play can't afford TPK easily. Make failure interesting rather than fatal.

**Defeat with Consequences**:

Instead of death, PC:
- **Captured**: Wake in enemy cell, must escape
- **Rescued**: NPC intervenes but PC owes debt
- **Driven Off**: Forced to retreat, enemy achieves goal
- **Pyrrhic Victory**: Win fight but lose something valuable (ally dies, evidence destroyed, corruption gained)

**Success at Cost**:

PC wins fight but:
- Exhaustion gained (level 1-2)
- Major resource expended (spell slots, daily abilities)
- Ally wounded (sidekick down, NPC needs rescue)
- Time lost (faction clock advances)
- Attention gained (enemies now hunt PC)

**Retreat Always an Option**:
- Make retreat mechanically viable (Dash action, difficult terrain, NPC cover)
- No shame in tactical withdrawal
- Retreating creates new complications (enemy escapes, reinforcements arrive, must find alternative path)

**Implementation**:
Before combat starts, identify:
- What happens if PC wins?
- What happens if PC loses?
- What happens if PC retreats?

All three should be interesting story outcomes.

### Control/Information Items

**Philosophy**: Give PC items that provide advantage in investigation/intrigue rather than raw combat power.

**Tier 1 Items (Levels 1-5)**:

**Seal of Maal** (Uncommon, requires attunement):
- Silver medallion with scales symbol
- **Effect**: Cast *Detect Thoughts* once per day (DC 13). Thoughts related to lies or injustice automatically detected.
- **Narrative Power**: Obvious holy symbol grants advantage on Persuasion with legal NPCs

**Fog Lantern** (Uncommon):
- Hooded lantern with blessed oil
- **Effect**: Light penetrates magical fog/mist. Creatures hidden by fog/mist within 30 ft. revealed.
- **Yllmourne Specific**: Essential for investigating cursed sites

**Sanctified Mallet** (Uncommon):
- Judge's gavel blessed by Maal
- **Effect**: +1 weapon. Against cursed or possessed creatures, deals extra 1d4 radiant damage and disadvantage on saves to resist banishment/exorcism.
- **Narrative Power**: Symbol of judicial authority

**Tier 2 Items (Levels 6-10)**:

**Covenant Scroll** (Rare, consumable):
- Ancient legal parchment
- **Effect**: Once per week, summon Maal's servant (use **Couatl** stat block, serves for 1 hour, won't fight but will testify to truth, provide guidance, or protect PC from single attack)

**Mirror of True Testimony** (Rare, requires attunement):
- Hand mirror showing past events
- **Effect**: Once per day, view past event that occurred within 30 ft. of mirror's current location (up to 7 days ago). Shows 1 minute of events. Can't be deceived by illusions.

**Chains of Binding Oath** (Rare):
- Silver manacles
- **Effect**: Creature bound by these chains (Strength DC 20 to break) must answer 3 questions truthfully (Wisdom save DC 16 to resist each question). After 3 questions, chains dissolve.

**Tier 3 Items (Levels 11-16)**:

**Tome of Precedent** (Very Rare, requires attunement):
- Leather-bound book of legal cases
- **Effect**: Grants expertise in Persuasion when arguing legal/ethical matters. Once per day, cite precedent to automatically succeed on Persuasion check (DM determines if situation qualifies as legal/ethical).

**Eye of Judgment** (Very Rare, requires attunement):
- Crystal monocle
- **Effect**: See alignment auras (gold = good, red = evil, blue = lawful, green = chaotic). *Detect Magic* at will. See through magical disguises.

**Tier 4 Items (Levels 17-20)**:

**Scales of Absolute Truth** (Legendary, requires attunement):
- Balance scales that weigh souls
- **Effect**: Once per day, force creature to speak absolute truth for 10 minutes (Wisdom save DC 19, advantage if creature willing). Lies cause 4d10 psychic damage to liar.

**Gavel of Final Verdict** (Legendary, requires attunement):
- Judge's gavel that echoes with divine authority
- **Effect**: +2 weapon. Once per day, declare verdict on creature (Charisma save DC 19):
  - **Guilty**: Creature stunned for 1 minute (save each turn to end)
  - **Innocent**: Creature healed for 8d8+20 HP and cleansed of curses
  - **Undecided**: Both PC and target gain advantage on next attack vs. each other

## Section 5: Divine Progression - Maal's Blessings

### Philosophy

The PC's connection to Maal, god of Law, Justice, and Balance, strengthens throughout the campaign. This progression represents growing divine favor earned through just actions, sacrifices for truth, and dedication to order.

**Narrative Arc**: PC begins as faithful servant, becomes recognized champion, eventually transcends to near-divine status as Hand of Maal.

**Mechanical Design**: Blessings supplement class abilities without replacing them. They provide investigation/social advantages fitting the campaign's themes.

### Blessing Tiers

#### Tier 1 (Levels 1-3): The Seeker

**Narrative Status**: Faithful servant investigating injustice. Maal watches with interest.

**Blessing Granted**: *Sense Falsehood*

**Mechanics**:
- **Frequency**: Once per day
- **Effect**: For 1 minute, PC knows when creature within 30 ft. speaks deliberate lie (not mistakes or ignorance). No save. Works even against magic that conceals lies (but doesn't overcome *Mind Blank*).
- **Limitation**: Doesn't reveal the truth, only confirms falsehood

**Acquisition**: Automatically granted after character creation or first significant act of justice (defending innocent, pursuing truth against opposition).

**Narrative Cues**:
- PC feels warmth when liars speak
- Scales symbol glows faintly
- Intuitive certainty, not magical detection

#### Tier 2 (Levels 4-6): The Investigator

**Narrative Status**: Proven dedication. Maal's name associated with PC's actions. Reputation growing.

**Blessing Granted**: *Vision of Truth*

**Mechanics**:
- **Frequency**: Once per long rest
- **Effect**: PC touches location or object and sees 1 minute of past events that occurred there (up to 7 days ago). PC chooses time viewed. Vision shows truth (ignores illusions, reveals disguises).
- **Limitation**: PC experiences vision as observer, can't interact or investigate during vision

**Acquisition**: Granted after Level 3 milestone + performing act that demonstrates investigative excellence (solving complex mystery, proving innocence, exposing corruption).

**Narrative Cues**:
- PC's vision overlays with ghostly images
- Voices echo from the past
- Sensation of standing outside time

**Retained Abilities**: PC keeps *Sense Falsehood* (now 2/day)

#### Tier 3 (Levels 7-9): The Arbiter

**Narrative Status**: Formal recognition by Maal's church. PC becomes religious figure as well as investigator.

**Blessing Granted**: *Summon Summary Hearing*

**Mechanics**:
- **Frequency**: Once per long rest
- **Effect**: PC creates 30 ft. radius zone of truth and compulsion. All creatures in zone must make Wisdom save (DC = 8 + proficiency + Charisma modifier) or compelled to answer questions truthfully and completely for 10 minutes. Creatures can't leave zone during this time (magical barrier). Save repeated at end of each turn to end (but can't leave until ended).
- **Limitation**: Creatures with legendary resistance can use it. Devils/fey gain advantage. Zone requires concentration.

**Acquisition**: Granted at Level 7 + completing major investigation (Act II climax: exposing Faylene's hex, preventing library destruction).

**Title Granted**: **Talesman of Maal** (formal religious rank)

**Narrative Cues**:
- Golden circle appears on ground
- Scales manifest in air above PC
- Divine authority palpable to all present

**Retained Abilities**: *Sense Falsehood* (3/day), *Vision of Truth* (1/day)

#### Tier 4 (Levels 10-12): The Champion

**Narrative Status**: Divine champion. Maal's direct agent in material world. Feared and respected.

**Blessing Granted**: *Scales of Justice*

**Mechanics**:
- **Frequency**: Once per day
- **Effect**: When making any d20 roll related to justice, truth, or lawful order, PC can invoke scales. Roll twice, take either result. If both rolls would succeed, gain inspiration.
- **Scope**: Applies to attack rolls (fighting criminals), saving throws (resisting corruption), ability checks (Investigation, Insight, Persuasion in legal contexts)
- **Limitation**: PC decides if action qualifies; DM has veto if clearly unrelated

**Acquisition**: Granted at Level 10 + becoming recognized threat to major conspiracy (Act III: discovering Sogmonnath, confronting planar agents).

**Narrative Cues**:
- Scales appear in PC's eyes
- Moment of perfect clarity
- Certainty of righteous action

**Retained Abilities**: All previous blessings with improved frequency

#### Tier 5 (Levels 13-16): The Emissary

**Narrative Status**: Cosmic representative. PC operates in extraplanar courts, negotiates with celestials and fiends.

**Blessing Granted**: *Planar Advocate*

**Mechanics**:
- **Frequency**: Permanent
- **Effect**:
  - Advantage on all Charisma checks when engaging in formal legal/diplomatic proceedings with extraplanar entities
  - Immune to charm effects in legal settings (courts, negotiations, contracts)
  - Can read and understand any written legal contract or law in any language
  - Extraplanar entities recognize PC's authority (treat as if PC had +5 Charisma for reaction purposes in formal settings)
- **Limitation**: Doesn't work in combat or informal situations. Requires procedural formality.

**Acquisition**: Granted at Level 13 + entering extraplanar courts for first time (Act IV begins).

**Narrative Cues**:
- Aura of authority visible to planar entities
- Celestial/infernal languages comprehensible
- Contracts glow when PC reads them

**Retained Abilities**: All previous blessings

#### Tier 6 (Levels 17-19): The Judge

**Narrative Status**: Near-divine authority. PC's judgments carry cosmic weight. Maal speaks directly through PC.

**Blessing Granted**: *Final Testimony*

**Mechanics**:
- **Frequency**: Once per day
- **Effect**: PC designates one creature within 60 ft. Target must speak absolute truth for 1 minute (no save, even legendary resistance doesn't apply). Target answers all questions fully and honestly. Target remembers and is aware but cannot resist.
- **Limitation**: Doesn't work on deities or entities with divine rank. Can be blocked by *Mind Blank* or similar epic magic.
- **Secondary Effect**: If target lies anyway (via magic or divine power), both PC and target make contested Charisma checks. Winner's reality becomes truth (defeated creature's lie is rewritten from reality or PC's compulsion fails).

**Acquisition**: Granted at Level 17 + performing act of ultimate justice (exposing cosmic conspiracy, judging extraplanar entity, preventing planar catastrophe).

**Narrative Cues**:
- PC's voice echoes with divine harmonics
- Scales of Maal manifest physically
- Reality bends toward truth

**Retained Abilities**: All previous blessings

#### Tier 7 (Level 20): Hand of Maal

**Narrative Status**: Epic apotheosis. PC is living avatar of Law and Justice. Destined for celestial service or divine ascension.

**Epic Boon Granted**: *Balance of Life and Death*

**Mechanics**:
- **Frequency**: Once per day
- **Effect**: PC can reverse death exactly as *True Resurrection* spell, no material components required. Can also choose to end any ongoing effect caused by divine/infernal/fey magic (equivalent to *Wish* used for dispelling).
- **Additional Powers**:
  - Immune to any magical effect that would prevent speaking truth
  - Cast *Zone of Truth* at will (DC = 8 + proficiency + Charisma modifier)
  - Ability Score Increase: +2 to Wisdom and Charisma (maximum 22)
  - Truesight 60 ft.

**Acquisition**: Automatically granted at Level 20 + completion of epic trial (Trial of the Seal, Act IV climax).

**Narrative Cues**:
- PC glows with golden radiance
- Scales symbol appears as halo
- Mortals feel compelled to honesty in PC's presence

**Retained Abilities**: All previous blessings

### Using Blessings in Play

**Acquisition Ceremonies**:
Each blessing tier comes with narrative moment:
- Vision from Maal (dream, divine messenger)
- Pilgrimage to sacred site
- Trial by ordeal
- Recognition ceremony by Maal's church

**Losing Blessings**:
PC can lose divine favor through:
- **Deliberate Injustice**: Knowingly punishing innocent, protecting guilty
- **Breaking Oaths**: Violating sworn promises or legal contracts
- **Corruption**: Reaching 8+ shadow corruption without seeking redemption
- **Denying Truth**: Deliberately lying about crucial matters

**Redemption**: If lost, blessings can be regained through:
- Confession and penance
- Act of supreme sacrifice for justice
- Divine trial to prove worthiness
- Completion of redemption quest

**Temporary Suppression**:
Certain locations suppress blessings:
- Infernal courts (devils' home advantage)
- Chaos realms (Naran's domains)
- Unholy sites (desecrated temples)

Suppression creates dramatic tension in key encounters.

**Mechanical Balance**:
- Blessings don't require attunement
- Don't consume action economy (most are abilities, not actions)
- Scale with character level naturally
- Enhance investigation/social pillar without breaking combat

## Section 6: Debate/Rhetoric Mechanics

### When to Use

**Appropriate Situations**:
- Public trials or formal hearings
- Political negotiations with faction leaders
- Theological disputes with Church authorities
- Defending accused from mob justice
- Convincing jury or council

**Inappropriate Situations**:
- Combat dialogue (use Intimidation/Persuasion as normal)
- Casual conversation
- Simple information gathering
- Situations where outcome predetermined

**Identifying Debate Scenes**:
- Stakes are high (life/death, policy change, reputation)
- Multiple witnesses/audience present
- Opposing viewpoint has legitimate argument
- Outcome uncertain based on PC's performance

### Structure: Chained Skill Tests

**Basic Framework**:

1. **Define Stakes**: What happens if PC wins? What if PC loses?
2. **Set Clock**: Typically 4-6 segments for PC to fill
3. **Opposition Clock**: Opponent fills own clock with successful arguments
4. **Victory Condition**: First to fill clock wins debate

**Skill Options**:
- **Persuasion**: Appeal to emotions, values, common good
- **Deception**: Strategic misdirection, withholding damaging truths
- **Intimidation**: Authority, consequences, force of personality
- **Insight**: Read opponent, counter arguments, exploit weaknesses
- **History**: Cite precedent, invoke tradition, demonstrate knowledge
- **Investigation**: Present evidence, build logical case
- **Performance**: Theatrical delivery, sway audience emotionally
- **Religion**: Theological arguments, invoke divine authority

**DC Scaling**:
- **Easy Audience** (sympathetic): DC 12-13
- **Neutral Audience**: DC 14-15
- **Hostile Audience**: DC 16-17
- **Expert Opposition**: DC 18-20

**Turn Structure**:
1. PC describes argument approach and rolls chosen skill
2. Opponent makes opposing check (contested) or PC rolls vs. DC
3. Success: Fill 1 segment on PC's clock
4. Failure: Opponent fills 1 segment on their clock
5. Critical success (beat DC by 5+): Fill 2 segments
6. Critical failure (fail by 5+): Opponent fills 2 segments

**Duration**: Each exchange = 1-5 minutes in-game time. Typical debate = 10-30 minutes.

### Complications

#### Truth vs. Social Consequences

**The Dilemma**: Sometimes truth is unpopular. PC must choose between winning debate and maintaining integrity.

**Mechanic**:
- **Telling Unpopular Truth**: DC increases by +3, but gain Maal's favor (blessing use refresh, corruption reduction, divine approval)
- **Strategic Deception**: Normal DC, but risk losing divine favor if egregious
- **Withholding Truth**: Normal DC, no divine consequence if not lying (but moral weight)

**Example**: Defending accused murderer whom PC knows is technically guilty but was defending self. Telling truth might lose case. Strategic argument focusing on self-defense law might win without lying.

**DM Guidance**: Allow PC to navigate this tension. Reward creative solutions that uphold both truth and justice.

#### Public Opinion

**Audience Reactions**: Crowd or jury's mood affects difficulty.

**Mechanic**:
- **Sympathetic Crowd**: Reduce DC by 2, opponent disadvantage
- **Hostile Crowd**: Increase DC by 2, opponent advantage
- **Swaying Crowd**: After each exchange, audience may shift

**Crowd Shift Triggers**:
- PC critical success: Crowd becomes more sympathetic (DC -1)
- PC critical failure: Crowd becomes more hostile (DC +1)
- Dramatic revelation: Major shift (DC -3 to +3)
- NPC ally speaks up: Temporary advantage on next check

**Tracking**: Use informal "crowd mood" indicator (-3 hostile to +3 sympathetic).

#### NPC Interruptions

**Allies**:
- Provide evidence (+2 to next Investigation-based argument)
- Testify as witness (automatic 1 segment fill if credible)
- Emotional appeal (advantage on next Persuasion check)

**Enemies**:
- Heckle (disadvantage on next check)
- Counter-testimony (opponent gains +2)
- Procedural challenges (PC must make additional check to continue)

**Mechanics**: NPCs can spend "influence" to intervene. Each major NPC has 1-3 influence points per debate. Once spent, they can't intervene again.

### Example: Public Trial (Act I Climax)

**Situation**: PC defends accused plague-spreader from mob lynching.

**Stakes**:
- **PC Victory**: Accused gets fair trial, PC establishes reputation as justice-bringer, mob disperses peacefully
- **PC Defeat**: Lynching occurs, PC injured trying to protect accused, mob becomes recurring threat

**Setup**:
- **PC Clock**: 6 segments
- **Mob Clock**: 6 segments
- **Starting DC**: 16 (hostile crowd)
- **Opposition**: Mob leaders make contested Charisma checks (+4 modifier)

**Skills Available**:
- **Persuasion DC 16**: "This person deserves trial! You're better than this mob!"
- **Intimidation DC 15**: "As agent of Maal, I will arrest anyone who touches the accused!"
- **Investigation DC 14**: "Look at the evidence—the plague timeline doesn't match!"
- **Insight DC 15**: Read mob leaders, identify who's truly angry vs. who's opportunistic
- **Religion DC 14**: "Gliriel teaches mercy! Maal demands justice, not vengeance!"

**Complications**:
- **Ally Interruption** (if sidekick present): Sidekick presents legal precedent (PC gains advantage on next History or Persuasion check)
- **Enemy Interruption** (mob agitator): "The investigator protects plague-spreaders!" (PC must make Persuasion DC 16 or suffer disadvantage on next check)
- **Crowd Shift**: If PC fills 3 segments, some mob members waver (DC reduces to 14)
- **Truth Dilemma**: Accused did break quarantine (minor crime). Admitting this increases DC by 2 but demonstrates honesty. Concealing it risks exposure (if mob discovers, auto-fill 2 segments on mob clock).

**Possible Flow**:

**Round 1**:
- PC: Persuasion check (rolled 18) vs. DC 16 = Success, fill 1 segment
- Mob Leader: Contested Persuasion (rolled 15 vs. PC's 18) = PC wins, fill 1 more segment (total: 2/6)

**Round 2**:
- PC: Investigation check to present evidence (rolled 12) vs. DC 14 = Failure by 2
- Mob fills 1 segment (Mob: 1/6)

**Round 3**:
- PC: Calls on sidekick for legal precedent (advantage on next check)
- PC: Persuasion with advantage (rolled 14 and 19, take 19) vs. DC 16 = Success, fill 1 segment (PC: 3/6)
- Crowd shifts (DC reduces to 14)

**Round 4**:
- Enemy interruption: Agitator shouts accusation
- PC: Must make Persuasion DC 16 (rolled 13) = Failure, disadvantage on next check

**Round 5**:
- PC: Religion check with disadvantage (rolled 16 and 8, take 8) vs. DC 14 = Failure by 6
- Critical failure: Mob fills 2 segments (Mob: 3/6)

**Round 6**:
- PC: Decides to tell truth about quarantine break but argue for proportional justice
- PC: Persuasion DC 16 (truth increases DC by 2, so DC 16) rolled 20 = Critical success!
- Fill 2 segments (PC: 5/6)
- Gain Maal's blessing refresh

**Round 7**:
- Mob Leader: Contested check (rolled 9 vs. PC's passive) = PC wins
- Fill 1 segment (PC: 6/6)

**Outcome**: PC victory! Accused given fair trial. Mob disperses, grumbling but respecting PC's authority.

**Reward**: Reputation established. Faction reputation with city government +1. Maal's blessing refreshed. Level up (2→3).

### Partial Outcomes

**Tied Clocks**: If both fill simultaneously, compromise:
- Accused gets trial but held in unsafe conditions
- Mob disperses but some splinter group plots revenge
- PC wins argument but suffers social consequence

**Abandoned Debate**: PC can withdraw (tactical retreat):
- Immediate loss but can try alternative approach
- Opponent achieves goal unopposed
- PC reputation suffers

### Milestone Details

**Act I (Levels 1-3): The Murder Mystery**
- **Duration**: 7-8 sessions
- **Focus**: Investigation fundamentals, faction introduction, plague discovery
- **Pacing**: Fast initial levels (1→2→3 within first quarter of campaign)

**Act II (Levels 4-7): The Conspiracy Deepens**
- **Duration**: 10-12 sessions
- **Focus**: Faction politics, curse revelation, library defense
- **Pacing**: Moderate (1 level per 3 sessions)

**Act III (Levels 8-12): The Crisis**
- **Duration**: 12-15 sessions
- **Focus**: Plague cure, planar discovery, major confrontations
- **Pacing**: Steady (1 level per 3-4 sessions)

**Act IV (Levels 13-20): The Cosmic Trial**
- **Duration**: 18-20 sessions
- **Focus**: Extraplanar courts, divine progression, epic conclusion
- **Pacing**: Epic tier pacing (1 level per 2-3 sessions for capstone feel)

### Adjusting Pacing

**If Campaign Moves Faster**:
- Combine milestones (defeat library plotters AND expose Faylene = jump from 4→6)
- Add bonus levels for exceptional play
- Accelerate Act IV if group wants faster conclusion

**If Campaign Moves Slower**:
- Insert side quests between milestones without leveling
- Split major milestones (discover Sogmonnath = level 11, understand its nature = level 12)
- Extend Acts II-III for deeper faction play

**Player Expectation Management**:
- Communicate at Session 0: "This is milestone-based, you'll level when story advances"
- Telegraph upcoming milestones: "Solving this mystery will mark your growth"
- Celebrate level-ups narratively (divine vision, training montage, reputation increase)

## Section 8: Rewards (Thematic Treasures)

### Philosophy

Yllmourne is urban investigation campaign, not dungeon crawl. Treasure should reflect this:
- **No Random Gold Hoards**: Wealth comes from salaries, rewards, faction payments
- **Meaningful Items**: Each item tied to story or investigation
- **Knowledge as Reward**: Information, contacts, legal authority often more valuable than gold
- **Reputation Matters**: Titles and faction standing open doors wealth can't

### Treasure Categories

#### Legal/Divine Items

**Legal Grimoires**:
- **Precedents of the Harbor Courts** (Uncommon, 500 gp value): +1 bonus to Persuasion checks when arguing maritime law
- **Codex of Sacred Law** (Rare, 2,000 gp value): +2 bonus to Persuasion in religious legal matters, advantage on Religion checks about divine law
- **The Balanced Scales Manuscript** (Very Rare, 10,000 gp value): +3 bonus to all Persuasion checks in formal legal proceedings, can cite passage to gain advantage on any Charisma check related to justice (1/day)

**Sanctified Tools**:
- **Blessed Incense of Truth** (Consumable, 100 gp per stick): Burn during interrogation, creatures have disadvantage on Deception checks for 10 minutes
- **Ceremonial Dagger of Maal** (Uncommon, 150 gp): +1 dagger, glows in presence of lies
- **Holy Symbol of the Balanced Scales** (Rare, requires attunement): +1 to spell save DC when casting divination or enchantment spells related to truth

**Divine Relics**:
- **Fragment of the First Contract** (Legendary, priceless): Ancient parchment from Maal's first covenant. Allows casting *Geas* once per week with no save (target accepts willingly or spell fails). Breaking geas incurs divine retribution (automatic failure on next save).

#### Investigation Tools

**Magnifying Lenses**:
- **Jeweler's Precision Lens** (Uncommon, 75 gp): Advantage on Investigation checks examining small details (documents, jewelry, wounds)
- **Lens of True Seeing** (Rare, 1,500 gp, requires attunement): See invisible marks, detect alterations to documents, identify magical inks (effectively *Detect Magic* on written materials only, at will)

**Alchemical Analysis Kits**:
- **Poison Detective's Kit** (Uncommon, 200 gp): Advantage on Medicine or Investigation checks to identify poisons, toxins, or plague symptoms. Contains 20 test strips (consumed on use).
- **Portable Laboratory** (Rare, 800 gp): Traveling alchemist's tools granting advantage on all Intelligence (Alchemist's tools) checks. Can create basic potions with half materials cost.

## Section 10: Encounter Design by Level

### Encounter Philosophy for Solo Play

- **Reduce Numbers**: Solo PC can't handle action economy of multiple foes
- **Increase Drama**: Fewer enemies but more interesting abilities
- **Environmental Factors**: Terrain, hazards, objectives beyond "kill all enemies"
- **Alternatives to Combat**: Many encounters should have social/stealth solutions

### Levels 1-2: Street Threats

**Appropriate Challenges**:
- **Dock Bandits**: 1-2 thugs (CR 1/2), environmental hazards (crates, water)
- **Plague Victims**: Swarm of rats (CR 1/4) + environmental (burning buildings)
- **Corrupt Guards**: 1 guard (CR 1/8) + 1 thug (CR 1/2) trying to shake down merchant
- **Investigation Traps**: Collapsing building (Dexterity save DC 13, 2d6 damage)

**Example Encounter - Dock Brawl**:
- **Setup**: 2 thugs shaking down witness PC needs to interview
- **Tactics**: Thugs use Multiattack (2 mace attacks), flank if possible
- **Solo Adjustment**: Reduce to 1 thug + 1 bandit (CR 1/8)
- **Environment**: Dock planks (Acrobatics DC 11 or fall into water—difficult terrain to climb out)
- **Alternative**: Intimidation DC 14 to make thugs back down without fight

**XP Budget**: 200-300 XP for Hard encounter (1-2 CR 1/2 creatures OR 1 CR 1 creature)

### Levels 3-4: Organized Crime

**Appropriate Challenges**:
- **Enforcer Gangs**: 1 veteran (CR 3) + environmental advantage (ambush, hostages)
- **Fog Cultists**: 1 cult fanatic (CR 2) + 1-2 cultists (CR 1/8)
- **Dock Puzzles**: Skill challenge to defuse trap or rescue hostage (avoid combat)
- **Ship Navigation**: Sailing challenge during storm (series of skill checks)

**Example Encounter - Cultist Hideout**:
- **Setup**: 1 cult fanatic + 1 cultist performing ritual
- **Tactics**: Fanatic casts *Hold Person*, cultist attacks restrained PC
- **Solo Adjustment**: Use sidekick to Help action (advantage on saves)
- **Environment**: Ritual circle (Arcana DC 14 to disrupt, ends fanatic's spellcasting)
- **Alternative**: Stealth DC 15 to sabotage ritual without alerting cultists

**XP Budget**: 600-900 XP for Hard encounter (1 CR 2-3 creature + minions OR environmental challenge)

### Levels 5-7: Faction Lieutenants

**Appropriate Challenges**:
- **Corrupt Constabulary**: 1 knight (CR 3) + environmental (crowd watching, legal consequences)
- **Brutal Lawyers**: 1 noble (CR 1/8) + 1 mage (CR 6) acting as "legal duel"
- **Naval Sabotage**: Crew battle on burning ship (clock-based challenge)
- **Curse Manifestations**: 1 green hag (CR 3) + environmental (cursed fog)

**Example Encounter - Legal Duel**:
- **Setup**: Opposing lawyer challenges PC to "trial by combat" (legal in Yllmourne for certain disputes)
- **Enemy**: 1 mage (representing opponent) + magical wards (environment)
- **Tactics**: Mage uses battlefield control (*Web*, *Hypnotic Pattern*), stays at range
- **Solo Adjustment**: Mage has 50% HP, focus on control over damage
- **Environment**: Courtroom wards prevent lethal damage (reduces to 1 HP instead of 0), first to drop "loses" case
- **Alternative**: Debate challenge (Section 6) instead of combat

**XP Budget**: 1,800-2,700 XP for Hard encounter (1 CR 5-6 creature OR multiple CR 3-4)

### Levels 8-10: Criminal Masterminds

**Appropriate Challenges**:
- **Mafia Champions**: 1 crime boss (bandit captain CR 2 + class levels to CR 7) + elite guards
- **Shipyard Constructs**: 1-2 animated armor (CR 1) + 1 helmed horror (CR 4)
- **Contested Healing Rituals**: Opposed skill challenge vs. dark priest (cleric NPC)
- **Thranengi Hunt**: Clock-based sea monster battle (see Section 4)

**Example Encounter - Thranengi**:
- **Setup**: Sea monster attacks ship during investigation voyage
- **Enemy**: Thranengi (custom stats: AC 16, HP 120, multiattack with tentacles)
- **Clock-Based**: 6-segment clock (see Section 4 for full mechanics)
- **Solo Adjustment**: Ship crew assists (fill 1 segment if PC rallies them)
- **Environment**: Ship combat (risk of falling overboard, damaged ship)
- **Alternative**: Negotiate using Covenant of First Landing (Persuasion DC 18)

**Custom Stat Block - Thranengi**:

- **AC** 16 (natural armor)
- **HP** 120 (16d10+32)
- **Speed** 10 ft., swim 60 ft.
- **STR** 18 (+4), **DEX** 12 (+1), **CON** 14 (+2), **INT** 10 (+0), **WIS** 13 (+1), **CHA** 8 (-1)
- **Skills** Perception +5, Stealth +5 (in water)

### Levels 11-13: Planar Agents

**Appropriate Challenges**:
- **Planar Emissaries**: 1 bearded devil (CR 3) + 2 imps (CR 1) OR 1 fey knight (use knight + fey abilities)

## Conclusion

These mechanics work together to create a cohesive solo investigation campaign:

- **Faction Clocks** create urgency and dynamic world
- **Investigation Mechanics** ensure player always has path forward
- **Shadow Corruption** adds supernatural stakes
- **Solo Combat Balance** keeps fights challenging but winnable
- **Divine Progression** rewards just play and scales to epic levels
- **Debate Mechanics** make social conflicts as engaging as combat
- **Milestone Leveling** ties progression to story
- **Thematic Rewards** reinforce investigation focus
- **Session Template** keeps DM organized
- **Encounter Design** provides appropriate challenges at every tier

**Final DM Advice**:
- **Flexibility**: These are tools, not constraints. Adjust as needed for your player.
- **Communication**: Discuss what's working and what isn't. Solo play requires close collaboration.
- **Failure Forward**: Make failure interesting, not frustrating. Every setback creates new opportunities.
- **Celebrate Justice**: This campaign is about a character fighting for truth. Honor that theme in mechanics and narrative.

May Maal's scales balance true.
