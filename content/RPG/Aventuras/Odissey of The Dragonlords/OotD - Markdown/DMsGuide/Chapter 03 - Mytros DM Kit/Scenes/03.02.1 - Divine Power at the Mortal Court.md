# 1. The Arrival of the Gods: Divine Power at the Mortal Court

After the heroes make their plea to Queen Vallus and she agrees this is the only viable solution, she turns to the center of the Throne Hall. Acastus begins to protest, “Vallus, what do you think you’re doing? You can’t simply summon them here!”, but his voice is abruptly silenced.

> The relentless sound of rain hammering the palace roof ceases in an instant. A deep, absolute silence falls upon the Throne Hall, so complete that one can hear one’s own breath. Then a single musical note — pure and crystalline as dawn — echoes through the chamber, seeming to come from nowhere and everywhere at once. The note vibrates in the air, calming hearts and dispelling fear. The doors of the Royal Hall swing open on their own, revealing **Kyrah**, Goddess of Music, lyre in hand and noble sorrow in her eyes. She enters, and the gentle melody follows her like a mantle.
>
> Immediately after, the air crackles with the scent of ozone and polished bronze. A subtle tremor runs across the marble floor, like the prelude to an earthquake. On the opposite side of the hall, a wavering distortion of heat manifests, solidifying into the imposing figure of **Pythor**, God of Battle. He does not wear full armor but the tunic of a warrior‑king, a sheathed sword at his side. His presence is a storm contained, a colossal power held in iron discipline.
>
> Finally, a wave of dry heat — like the breath of a forge — sweeps the hall. A fragrance of coal, hot metal, and stone emanates from Acastus’ very throne. From behind him, out of shadows no mortal fire could cast, emerges **Volkan**, God of the Forge. He is shorter than the others, but his stance is that of a mountain. The hands of a craftsman are calloused; his eyes gleam with the light of an ancient hearth.

# 2. The Court Reacts: Stirring the Snake’s Nest

The gods’ arrival transforms politics into a direct confrontation of power.

- King Acastus: Shaken; the façade slips, replaced by incredulity and barely contained anger. He rises, but seems smaller before the divine. He has lost control in his own court, and his look to Vallus is a blend of fury and resentment.
- Commander Gaius: Visceral. He stares not with fear, but fanatical hatred. To him these are “Usurpers,” enemies of his true master, Sydon. Jaw clenched, hand on hilt.
- Chondrus: Smiles — subtle, malicious delight. He savors the chaos: the king’s humiliation, the raw power of gods — a delicious spectacle and an indirect offering to Lutheria.
- Bella and the Centurions: Bella pales; the centurions stand rigid, discipline barely masking awe.

# 3. The Miracle Rite: A Song Against the Storm

Vallus ignores Acastus and addresses her siblings and the heroes. “Sydon’s wrath threatens to destroy the city our father helped build and our mother blessed. We must act — together.”

Pythor: “It will have a cost — a great cost.”

Kyrah: “But the cost of doing nothing is annihilation. The Oracle’s Chosen have guided us. We must listen.”

The gods do not need to go to the Temple; their power is with them. They move to the center of the Throne Hall and form a circle.

> The ritual begins.
>
> **Volkan** is the anchor. He strikes the floor, and a low hum resonates from the stone foundations of the palace, rising up the columns like sap through an ancient tree. He draws strength from the land itself — from the bedrock of Mytros.
>
> **Pythor** raises a hand; an invisible aura of pure martial will emanates, a bulwark of indomitable resolve. Not a physical shield, but the very essence of order and discipline pushing back the Titan’s chaotic fury.
>
> **Kyrah** plucks her lyre; the melody that was once gentle now swells into a mighty antiphon. Her music does not fight the storm’s sound, but harmonizes it, weaving a counter‑melody that finds the hurricane’s dissonances and resolves them into peaceful chords.
>
> **Vallus** closes her eyes; threads of silver light — like pure thoughts — extend from her, connecting the others. She is the weaver, the strategist, guiding and interlacing her siblings’ raw powers into a spell of complexity incomprehensible to mortals.
>
> Outside, the citizens of Mytros witness a miracle. A dome of golden, silver, and bronze light forms over the city, emanating from the Great Palace. The light pushes back the black clouds. Sydon’s face, wrought in the storm, roars in silent frustration before being unraveled and dispersed. The downpour becomes a gentle drizzle, and then ceases. A sunbeam pierces the clouds, illuminating the soaked city.

# 4. Immediate and Lasting Consequences

- For the Gods: The cost is visible. Kyrah pales; Pythor leans on his sheathed sword; Volkan looks older; Vallus is composed but fragile. Each suffers a permanent level of exhaustion — a portion of their divine spark sacrificed.
- Politics and Society: A devastating blow to Acastus and the Order of Sydon. Mytros saw not their king solve the crisis, but the Five work a miracle at the behest of the Oracle’s Chosen. Faith surges. Acastus must congratulate the heroes, but jealousy grows. Gaius and the Order look foolish or impotent — fueling their fanaticism.
- For the Heroes: Their fame explodes. They become “Friends of the Gods,” instruments of a miracle. Immense respect from citizens and the Five’s faithful — and mortal enemies to the Order of Sydon and to Acastus’ envy.
