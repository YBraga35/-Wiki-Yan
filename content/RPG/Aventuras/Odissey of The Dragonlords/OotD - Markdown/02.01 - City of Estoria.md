# Chapter 2 : The Great Tasks

> “I miss my mother so much, but I wish she’d never created the Pact of Peace. Five hundred years without a battle to test me — five hundred years without a real victory. It’s worse than anything the Titans could have imagined to torment me.”  
> – *Pythor, King of Estoria, God of Battle*

The heroes rescued the Oracle from the clutches of the witch Heleka and, in the process, learned the first part of what the Destinies have in store for them. They must now carry out a series of Great Tasks to prepare for the confrontation with the Titans.

## The Great Tasks

The Oracle Versi tells the heroes that, to face the Titans, they must strengthen themselves. She reveals the prophecy: they must accomplish **two or three** of the following tasks:

- **Claim the Weapons of the Dragon Lords:** Journey to the necropolis of Telamok in the Mithral Mountains.  
- **Ignite the Forges of Mithral:** Locate the ancient dwarven forge that the Rurik Clan in Estoria knows about.  
- **Drink from the Horn of Balmytria:** Find the Horn, which is said to be in the Dragon Sanctuary of Estoria.

# The City of Estoria

Estoria is the largest settlement along the Arkelon River. Built within a rocky gorge, it spreads from a stone fortress that faces the steppes to the north. The Arkelon itself runs along the northern edge of the fortress, forming a natural moat. Across the river, centaur patrols roam the pastures.

## Arrival at the Cracked Jewel

The journey eastward, toward Estoria, is a grim lesson in a Titan’s cruelty. What should have been a pastoral route has become a landscape of despair. The air grows drier and heavier with each step, carrying the scent of dust and finality. The land, once a fertile carpet, is now a cracked crust that crumbles beneath boots. Runic beds are stone‑scarred, and cattle carcasses lie scattered, their bones bleached by the relentless sun. Sydon’s influence is not a raucous storm, but a suffocating silence under a perfect‑blue, cruel sky.

Along the way you encounter a family pulling a rattling cart. The father, **Dymas**, gazes with deep, empty eyes.

> **Dymas:** “Heading for Estoria? Pray that the gods hear you, for they have forgotten us. The land has died. We’re going to Mytros… or anywhere where water still remembers how to run.”

### Arrival at the Cracked Jewel

From afar, Estoria gleams like a jewel nestled in a rocky gorge. To the north, an imposing fortress rises against the vast steppes, its walls kissed by the Arkelon River. From the fortress, the city cascades, a labyrinth of terracotta roofs.

But as you approach the southern gate, the illusion shatters. The air carries the stench of dust, sweat, and fear. The gate guards, **Lyco** and **Orestes**, are weary veterans.

> **Lyco** (leaning on his spear, voice hoarse): “Travelers? Welcome to the city of the heart. Bring your own drink; the wells are nearly dry, and wine is rationed to the gods… or the dogs of their guard.”  
> **Orestes** (hand near his sword): “King Pythor receives no visitors. The city is under the protection of Sydon’s Order. The Dragon Prison tavern is the least likely place to stir trouble. Keep quiet.”

## The Imminent War

For centuries, Estoria has been the greatest defense against the dangers of the steppes, yet it is now in decline. King‑God Pythor drowns his sorrows in wine, and panic spreads among the citizens.

- The herds have been decimated by the daily sacrifices demanded by Sydon.  
- A severe drought, wrought by the Storm‑Lord, plagues the region.  
- Cyclopes have been pillaging farms along the road to Mytros.  
- Centaurs, led by **Baenor**, gather in record numbers, preparing for an attack.  
- Sydon has demanded the sacrifice of the king’s daughter, Princess **ora**, at the next full moon.  
- King Pythor has gone mad with grief over his daughter.

## Key Locations in Estoria

### E1. Public Amphitheater

The circular stage of the outdoor theater was built at the base of a hill. It is used for festivals, plays, and major public announcements, including the famed annual wine festival.

- **Event: The Wine Festival** – If the crisis is resolved, Pythor will declare a festival. Priests of Lutheria, led by **Tassos**, arrive with fey performers. The festival lasts a week and features three plays: *The Fall of Estor*, *The Supplication of Pellenia*, and *Pythor and the Five Sisters*. The plays offer a chance for the heroes to learn about Thylean mythology, but also bring danger. Tassos’s lead actress, **Seema**, is a disguised Green Witch who will try to seduce one hero to kill him and devour his heart.

### E2. North Gate

This gate includes a fortified stone bridge that crosses the Arkelon River. The atmosphere is tense, with disciplined soldiers facing brash centaur raiders on the other side of the river. Heavy wooden doors remain closed and locked at all times.

### E3. South Gate

This gate is bustling with merchants and travelers. Most enter and exit the city through it, traveling the main road to the Central Lands. It is guarded by two bored‑looking soldiers, **Lyco** and **Orestes**.

- **Hook (Woodhike):** Rumors among merchants mention the halfling enclave of Woodhike to the south and its problems with bandits. A merchant may say that the famed cider, the “Sun‑Tears,” has stopped arriving, a drink that King Pythor himself prized for its nostalgic taste of older Thylea.

### E4. East Gate

This gate is quiet, leading eastward toward the vineyards and gardens that supply the city. The area is well protected by surrounding rock cliffs, so guards are rarely found here.

### E5. Agora (Central Market)

The heart of Estoria is a plaza where life tries to mimic normalcy. Colorful silk stalls offer textiles that no one purchases, and potters display jars to store water that does not exist. Conversations are whispered and glances furtive. Here rumors become truths: centaurs meet; sacrifices decimate herds; and the sternest demand— the sacrifice of Princess Anora.

- **Path of the Cursed:** An amulet vendor, **Pindar**, notices a mark on the Cursed. He pulls the hero into a dim alley, eyes wide with fear and admiration.  
  > “That mark… I saw it in an ancient scroll, one that spoke of a lineage touched by the Twin Titans, a curse forged in the very Thief‑of‑Typhoon Tunnel. The guardian of Telamok, the lich Damon… he was there when the curse was woven. He knows the truth.”

### E6. Residential Neighborhoods

Three distinct groups of buildings are walled off from the rest of the city: the soldiers’ barracks, the artisans’ workshops, and the farmhands’ rustic homes.

### E7. Dragon Sanctuary

A modest stone sanctuary with a green copper dome. Its guardian, **Aesop**, an elf of a melancholy demeanor and deep wisdom, is a historian and confidant of the gods. The air smells of cool incense and an ancient sadness.

> **Aesop:** “The Chosen of the Oracle. Versi has placed a heavy burden on their shoulders. I have seen such burdens crush men better than gods.”  
> He confirms that one of the Great Tasks is to drink from the Horn of Balmytria. His face tightens in pain, and he points to an empty marble pedestal.  
> > “The Horn… was stolen. Goat‑like creatures, servants of a darkness that lurks in the Ancient Forest, took it two nights ago.”

- **Investigation:** The broken relic reveals hoof prints and stains of an odd, dark wine. The trail leads to the Ancient Forest, pointing toward the **Mossy Temple**.  
- **Path of the Haunted:** Upon seeing the Haunted Hero, Aesop halts, his eyes fixed on the relic the hero carries.  
  > “That piece… it carries a touch of a magic I haven’t felt since the First War. Demetria, the dryad now corrupted in the Ancient Forest, gathers artifacts of lost lineages. Perhaps… perhaps she possesses something that belongs to her home.”

### E8. Dragon Prison Tavern

A boisterous, smoky refuge. The barkeep, **Ulfgar**, a white‑haired veteran, serves drinks and info with the same tired expression. Soldiers roll dice, while farmers stare into their mugs, seeing only the reflection of their dead fields.

- **Path of the Dragon Slayer:** In a dark table, dwarves of the Rurik Clan, led by **Borin**, drink in bitter silence. If approached, Borin slams the mug.  
  > “Mithral! They speak of the copper wealth of Estoria, but it’s slag compared to what we had. Lutheria cursed our mines, unleashed a fire beast and death. Yet the Mithral Forge still lies there, dormant. They say the Forge Guardian, an ancient salamander, still lives. She helped Volkan forge weapons that could kill gods… and dragons. She knows secrets to forge steel that pierces old scales.”

- **Mission (Mithral Mines):** The dwarves do not wish to return, but ask the heroes to recover the skulls of fallen kin from the mines for a proper burial, offering 10 p each.

### E9. Volkan’s Hall

Near the city center stands a large fortified building. Inside, the warm glow of embers and the hot air greet visitors. **Volkan**, a surly dwarf at his anvil, is in fact the God of Smithing and father of Pythor.

- **Mission (Mithral Mines):** Volkan recognizes the Chosen of the Oracle. He laments the silence of his greatest creation, the Mithral Forge, cursed by Lutheria. He promises to join the heroes in the mines once they rekindle its flame, forging for them weapons worthy of their destiny.  
- **Path of the Demigod:** For his grandson (Pythor’s son), Volkan is more direct.  
  > “Your father… always grandiose in ambition, yet short‑sighted. He began a weapon, one that could change the course of war, but never finished it. Only at the Mithral Forge can it be completed. Bring me his hammer and the right components, and I’ll forge a weapon that even a proud god will fear.”

### E10. The King’s Palace

The former great Palace of Estor now hovers over the city, a ghost of its former glory. The heroes’ reputation precedes them, and a royal escort summons them to the palace. In the throne room, **Pythor**— the God of Battle— is sunk deep in his seat, the despair engraved on his face.

> **Pythor:** (His voice a thunder muted by pain) “They want her. Sydon… that monster demands my daughter, **Anora**. A at the Rock of Estor to end the drought. My advisors, led by that dog of Sydon, **Gaius**, insist I obey. The Pact of Peace ties me. I am a war god who cannot fight. You… you are her only hope.”  
> You encounter **Anora**, not a defenseless maiden but a young woman with lion‑heart courage.  
> > “A king must protect his people, and a princess must protect her king. If my blood brings rain, let it be so. Fear for my father. This pain will destroy him.”

#### Path of the Demigod: A Burden of Blood

To his son, Pythor offers a chance at mutual redemption, his eyes showing a spark of hope for the first time in years.

> **Pythor:** “In you, I see the strength taken from me. Save your sister, my Anora, and I’ll give you my hammer, the tool that forged legends. With it, and with help from my father, **Volkan**, the cranky smith in the city center, you can finish the weapon I never could. A weapon worthy of a new war god. Do what I cannot. Be the hero I should have been.”

#### Additional Hooks in the Palace

- **Path of the Vanished:** When seeing the Vanished, a shadow of conflict sweeps through Gaius’s face, the commander of Sydon’s Order. He is a direct descendant of the hero and feels a conflicted bond to his legacy. He keeps the hero’s legendary armor, taken from the tomb at Telamok, believing it his rightful inheritance. He will challenge him, not only for duty to Sydon but to prove he himself is worthy of that legacy.  
- **Path of the Lost:** Noticing the hero hails from afar, Pythor may offer a specific deal.  
  > “And for you, coming from overseas… if you save my daughter, I’ll tell you what I know about the lost treasure of the Dragon Lords. They say the last holder was Estor Arkelander, and his ghost ship, the Ultros, still sails the cursed seas.”

### E11. The Rock of Estor

Rising from the shallow waters of the Arkelon, the Rock of Estor is a flat stone slab used as a sacrifice altar. A iron stake is planted in its center. Here, the basilisk of Sydon emerges to devour offerings. At dusk, a sphere of magical darkness envelops the rock, vanishing at dawn.

### E12. The North Steppes

The lands north of Estoria are too perilous to traverse. Banded centaur patrols roam the pastures. Occasionally a cyclops appears in the area.

### E13. Estonian Vineyards

East of the city, the dry, broken vineyards of **Thericles Phobas** paint a picture of Estoria’s struggle. Thericles, a descendant of the Dragon‑Lady Rizon Phobas, tends vines as if they were a beloved soul in a dying bed.

- **Mission (Mossy Temple):** Thericles is desperate.  
  > “My daughter… Corina. She and her friends were lured by Demetria, an impossible‑beauty woman in the Ancient Forest. She promised them secrets, power… and they followed her into darkness. Now, they’ve vanished.” He offers a **Thunder Javelin** as a reward for rescuing his daughter, providing a direct hook to the Mossy Temple mission.

### E14. Woodhike, Halfling Enclave

Following a narrow trail to the wooded hills, you reach Woodhike, an enclave of gnomes and halflings. The mayor, **Thobos**, is friendly but worried.

- **Mission (Bandits):** Recently, animals have been missing. Thobos suspects human poachers nearby. In reality, a band of outlaws led by the cruel **Korteva**, a former acolyte of Sydon turned Titan, enslaved a gentle cyclops named **Brontes**. Freeing Brontes and defeating Korteva can earn the heroes valuable allies and restore the famed Woodhike cider, the “Sun‑Tears.”

## Part 3: The Palace of Pain and the Prophecy Knot

The heroes’ reputation precedes them, and a royal escort summons them to the palace. It is a ghost of its former glory, with faded tapestries and trophies coated in dust. In the throne room, **Pythor**, the God of Battle, sits sank in his seat, a cup of wine in his hand, despair etched on his face.

> **Pythor:** (His voice a thunder muted by suffering) “They want her. Sydon… that monster demands my daughter, **Anora**. A sacrifice at the Rock of Estor to end the drought. My advisors, led by that dog of Sydon, **Gaius**, insist I obey. The Pact of Peace binds me. I am a war god who cannot fight. You… you are her only hope.”  
> You encounter **Anora**, not a defenseless maiden but a young woman with lion‑heart courage.  
> > “A king must protect his people, and a princess must protect her king. If my blood brings rain, let it be so. Fear for my father. This pain will destroy him.”

### The Prophecy of Estoria and Its Wordplay

At the center of the conflict lies an ancient prophecy that the Order of Sydon interprets literally and horribly.

> “When the sun wounds the earth, and the heavens fall silent in protest,  
> an old covenant awakens, sealed in stone and blood.  
> The chosen must be pure, born of thunder and lament,  
> and delivered to the Rock of Estor at dawn.  
>  
> A sacrifice of soul and flesh,  
> the earth soaking in courage and royal blood,  
> and then, only then, the drought will break from the first thunder’s breath.”

Many Greek tales revolve around tricks. Deceiving the gods by obeying the letter of the law is an ancient tradition. The prophecy is deliberately ambiguous, fertile ground for cunning.

- **“Chosen must be pure”** – purity may refer to intent rather than body. Perhaps it means someone with a selfless heart, whose motivations to save the city are genuine, contrasting with political ambition. Or “pure of a specific crime,” such as never shedding innocent blood. A literal virgin interpretation is simple but questionable.  
- **“Born of thunder and lament”** – “thunder” clearly references Pythor, the God of Battle. “Lament” is subtler. It may refer to the city’s collective sorrow under the drought, making any child of Estoria a candidate. It could also describe Anora, born beneath her father’s despair. The phrase’s duality suggests the sacrifice need not be royal alone but someone embodying both power and pain.  
- **“Delivered to the Rock”** – “Delivered” is not synonymous with “executed.” The prophecy doesn’t demand death, but an act of submission. It could mean binding to the rock for a set time, a lone vigil at dawn to dusk. Submission to the rock’s peril could suffice. A gift, not an end.  
- **“Sacrifice of soul and flesh”** – A “soul” sacrifice might mean facing fear or faith. A “flesh” sacrifice could be a single drop of blood, or a wound in a ritual battle. It’s a plea for proof of courage and resilience, not a life.  
- **“Royal blood”** – Gaius, in his motive, focuses on Anora’s lineage, but “royal blood” in Thylea could include any noble lineage, divine descent, or heroic mantle. It undermines the Order of Sydon’s certainty.

## Part 4: The Cast of Drama

### General Gaius, the Dog of Sydon

The commander of Sydon’s Order is a fervent, cold‑hearted pragmatist. He believes sacrifice is a necessary evil to prevent total annihilation. He feels no pleasure in it, yet his devotion to Sydon is absolute. He can present the prophecy in different ways, depending on the audience:

- **(Serene Version):** “The Rock of Estor cries for a worthy sacrifice. By command of Sydon, I offer Anora. May her act bring rain—or her lack doom all.”  
- **(Provocative for the Heroes):** “They call for royal blood, proclaim courage—prove it! The Rock awaits. Bind yourselves, not Anora’s, proof your worth.”  
- **Hook (The Vanished):** Seeing the Vanished, Gaius’s face tenses. He is a direct descendant of the hero and feels a conflicted bond. He keeps the hero’s legendary armor, taken from Telamok’s tomb, believing it his rightful inheritance. He will challenge him, not only to prove his own worth.

### Baenor, the Storm of the Steppes
#### Arrival of the Heroes at the Centaur Camp

The heroes approach the camp across golden steppes. Hundreds of leather tents spread across the horizon, perfumed with horse sweat and smoke. The centaur march appears almost ritualistic, breathing as one.

Baenor stands outside the camp, a monumental figure against stormy sky. His oiled torso gleams. He holds a double‑blade. He gazes at Estoria, as if beyond time.

He does not turn immediately. He remains still as a living statue until the heroes announce themselves. His voice sounds like a distant thunder, heavy and judgmental.

> **Baenor:** “More children of the ‘gods.’ You’ve come crawling from the stone city to plead with the storm? Your prayers are weak. The sky no longer listens.”

If the heroes mention the prophecy or Oracle:

> **Baenor:** “The chatter of Sydon’s daughter does not interest me. The sky has its own laws. The land’s drought needs tribute. A tribute that your ‘king‑god’ dares not pay. Like an old dog that forgets how to bark.”

#### The Challenge Offer

At the slightest negotiation, Baenor raises a calm hand, silent, his eyes burning with impatience.

> **Baenor:** “Words are wind. The pasture responds to steel and armor. You claim to be heroes? Speak of bravery? Prove it.”

> “If a king’s blood is too precious, may it be shed on the Rock. Survive Sydon’s present by dawn. If you do this… maybe, maybe I’ll hear you. The princess joins you by dusk.”  
> He points his jaw toward a stone rise marked by ancient symbols blood stains darkened by time.

> **Baenor:** “The Rock waits. Show me you are more than tales sung by drunkards.”

#### During the Challenge

Baenor does not fight. He watches from a nearby hill, surrounded by his tentatives. His eyes don’t blink, his laugh is absent.

If a hero performs something impressive:

> **Baenor** (to a hero): “That one fights like a true ___—”

If a hero falls:

> **Baenor**: “Weakness is purged. The Rock takes your tribute.”

If the heroes succeed:

Silence. No applause. A dignified hush that precedes a storm. Then Baenor descends the slope, walking slowly toward the exhausted, blood‑stained heroes.

#### Final Offer

Baenor observes them anew. Disdain turns into the old‑timed respect among warriors.

> **Baenor:** “You bled on sacred ground. You fought with fury my people almost forgot. You have strength. Perhaps… even honor.”  
> (He watches in silence, eyes probing their souls.)  
> **Baenor:** “The prophecy will be fulfilled. The debt will be paid. The gods… ah, they love wordplay. And I too know how to play.”  
> “The sacrifice of a virgin princess is poetic. But the surrender of a king, a ‘god,’ is a greater tribute. One that echoes through ages.”  
> “Help me seize Estoria. Forbid Pythor from yielding. May he bow to true Thylea, not a dying era.”  
> (He offers the other option.)  
> **Baenor:** “Or… hand over the girl now. Her blood will end drought as the old custom says. The choice is yours, heroes. Show me what legend you wish to become.”

## Part 5: Hooks, Surroundings, and Consequences

- **Woodhike and the Halflings:** To the south, Woodhike’s halfling enclave is terrorized by bandits led by cruel **Korteva**, who enslaved a gentle cyclops. Freeing him could earn allies and restore the famed “Sun‑Tears” cider.  
- **Wine Festival:** If the crisis ends, Pythor will declare a festival. It is a chance to meet **Tassos**, Lutheria’s priest, and his subversive theatrical works, and perhaps face the deadly Green Witch, **Seema**.  
- **Rock of Estor:** The rock itself is enigmatic. At twilight, a sphere of magical darkness surrounds it, disappearing at dawn. Basilisks of Sydon emerge there to feast. A basilisk’s bile may be key to reversing petrification.  
- **Consequences of Failure:** If the sacrifice is unfulfilled, the drought persists, slowly destroying Estoria. Pythor, broken, will abdicate; the city will fall to centaurs when the Pact of Peace ends.
