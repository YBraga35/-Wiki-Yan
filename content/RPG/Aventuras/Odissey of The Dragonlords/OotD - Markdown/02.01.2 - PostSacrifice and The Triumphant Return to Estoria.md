# The Triumphant Return to Estoria

## Scene 1: The Celebration of the Unbroken

The heroes’ return from the Rock of Estor is no quiet affair. Word of their survival travels faster than the Arkelon River, a spark of hope in a city stifled by fear. As they pass through the South Gate, they are not met by the same weary, cynical guards, but by a crowd that gathers spontaneously.

The air, once heavy with dust and despair, now vibrates with murmurs of awe and admiration. People emerge from their homes and workshops, faces scarred by drought and worry, now contorting into a blend of disbelief and joy.

> A blacksmith, face smeared with soot, drops his hammer and shouts to the crowd:  
> **“They’re back! The Chosen of the Oracle faced the Rock and did not bend!”**  
>  
> A young woman, whose brothers were taken by the centaurs, cuts through the throng and offers the heroes a small desert flower—the only one she managed to cultivate. Her eyes shine with tears.  
> **“You’ve given us hope. Perhaps the gods have not forgotten us after all.”**

At the Public Amphitheatre, King Pythor awaits them. He is not seated on his throne, but standing in the centre of the stage. The wine has been set aside. His eyes, once clouded by pain and alcohol, now burn with the ancient flame of the God of Battle that Thylea had almost forgotten. When he sees the heroes, he lifts his arms and his voice reverberates with a power that silences the city.

> **Pythor:**  
> “People of Estoria! For months we have lived under the shadow of the storm. We offered our livestock, our wine, our fear. And the Titan demanded the most precious thing. But today… today mortals rose where a god was chained! They did not break! Therefore, I decree a week of celebration! Let it be known as the Festival of the Unbroken! Burn the remaining carcasses in honour of the heroes, not the tyrant of the seas! Open the last barrels of wine! Today Estoria does not weep. Today Estoria fights!”

A deafening roar echoes down the gorge. The celebration is cathartic. Songs that had not been heard in months are played, and people dance in the streets, sharing what little they have.

## Scene 2: Legends Born (Fame 4)

During the Festival of the Unbroken, the tales of the heroes spread like wildfire across the dry grass, quickly becoming legends. Bards, inspired by Kyrah, the goddess of music, begin composing songs that give the heroes mythic epithets. Each title reflects not only what they did on the Rock, but the very essence of their Epic Path.

These are not mere nicknames; they are the opening verses of a new Thylean epic, sung in taverns and noble halls.

  _“…and the people call him the **Lion of Estoria**, because his roar of challenge on the sacred rock silenced the thunder of Sydon itself, proving he is the true son of the God of Battle.”_

- **To Elion, the Vanished One (The Vanished One):**  
  _“He is the **Specter of Estor**, who looked into the abyss and did not blink, for he has seen the face of eternity and returned to guide us, a reborn Dragon‑Lord.”_

- **To Basil, the Doomed One (The Doomed One):**  
  _“The harpies screamed his name, the basilisk feared him. He is the **Three‑Times‑Blessed**, the one who walks in death’s shadow and laughs, for he has outwitted inevitability more than once.”_

- **To Zephyr, the Haunted One (The Haunted One):**  
  _“Not even the river spirits dared touch him. The people call him **Bearer of Souls**, for the dead of his forgotten lineage know and respect the burden he silently carries.”_

- **To Yelena, the Exiled One (The Exile):**

## Scene 3: The Proposal in the Rain

On the third day of the festival, something miraculous happens. Grey clouds that had not been seen in almost a year gather above Estoria. Then a rain begins—not a violent storm from Sydon, but a gentle, constant downpour that washes away the dust from the city and promises life to the parched earth. People rush to the streets, lifting their faces to the sky, crying with joy.

Amid this celebration, a palace guard finds the heroes. “The King summons you. And… he asks you to bring the guardian of the Dragon Sanctuary.”

In the palace’s great hall, Pythor awaits. Beside him stand a sombre‑set Gaius and an Aesop whose ancient eyes glitter with new light. The rain taps softly against the high windows.

> **Pythor:** (His voice grave, the weight of centuries in his words)  
> “This rain… it is not my doing. It is a sign. A sign that the winds of destiny are shifting, and you are the storm that is coming. The Oracle has chosen you. I saw you challenge my despair. Now I ask you. The Pact of Peace is dying. The First War never truly ended. Thylea needs its Dragon‑Lords again.”  
>  
> He gestures toward Aesop, who steps forward.  
> **Aesop:** (his voice a whisper, like dry leaves in the wind)  
> “For five‑hundred years I have kept the stories. I have seen glory turn to dust and hope to despair. But in you, I see the echo of the past and the promise of the future. The King tells the truth. The Great Tasks the Oracle gave you… claiming the weapons inamok, re‑igniting the Mithral Forge, drinking the Horn… these are not merely tests. They are the steps to forge a new era. To become the new Dragon‑Lords.”  

Gaius, who had remained silent, finally speaks, his voice as cutting as ice.

> **Gaius:**  
> “Do not be deceived. This rain is an anomaly. The Storm‑Lord has not been defeated. He was… surprised. His wrath now ferments. Because you survived, you did not bring peace. You declared war. Every step you take on your ‘glorious path’ will be watched. And Sydon is no god that forgives.”  

Gaius turns and leaves, leaving his threat hanging in the air.

## Scene 4: The Steppes Messenger

As soon as Gaius departs, a guard rushes in, bowing before Pythor. “My King, a centaur approaches from the north gate. He comes alone, under a truce flag, and asks to speak with the… Unbroken.”  

Pythor looks at the heroes, a rare genuine smile on his face. “It seems your legend has already crossed the river.”  

At the fortified bridge of the North Gate, beneath the rain still falling, a young centaur from the Lance‑of‑Fury tribe awaits them. He dismounts—an act of respect—and kneels.

> **Scout Centaur:**  
> “I bring a message from my chief, **Baenor**. He says: *‘The army of Sydon is a plague over our lands, a promise of power wrapped in chains. We saw what they did at the Rock. We saw the rain. We saw that there is a force in Thylea that does not bow to Titans. If you are the true storm coming, then perhaps it is time for the free tribes to decide which side of the wind they will ride. Meet us on the Howling Wind Hills in three days if you wish to talk alliance.’*”  
>  
> The scout hands a carved tribal totem—an assured passage through their lands—then gallops back into the misty steppe.

### Continuation of the Adventure

The heroes now stand at a crucial junction:

- **The King’s Proposal:** Pythor has offered full support for them to complete the Great Tasks and become the new Dragon‑Lords. He can provide maps, resources, and Volkan’s wisdom.  
- **Aesop’s Insight:** He can detail the history behind each Great Task, the legends of the Dragon‑Lords’ weapons, and the dangers of Telamok’s necropolis.  
- **Gaius’s Threat:** The Order of Sydon now sees them as declared enemies. They will be hunted.  
- **Baenor’s Opportunity:** Aligning with the centaurs could be vital, but also perilous. Meeting them is a risk; ignoring them may create a powerful foe.  
- **The Festival:** The city is in revelry. It provides an opportunity to interact with the populace, earn more fame, perhaps resolve small tasks (such as the Woodhike bandits, now that civic morale is high), and face Lutheria’s agents during the wine festival.  

The rain continues to fall, cleansing old Estoria and preparing the ground for whatever the heroes decide to plant next.```
