# The Tome of Lutheria

The book discovered in Demetria’s chambers is more than a simple grimoire; it is an artifact imbued with the chaotic and malevolent essence of the Titan Lutheria.

## Appearance and Material

- **Binding:** The tome is sewn in human skin, stitched with sinews. On touch, the leather feels smooth yet disturbingly warm. The cover bears no title, only a vague, stretched face with no discernible features, as if the victim’s soul is trapped within. A successful Wisdom (Insight) check, DC 14, reveals that the skin once belonged to an elf woman.

- **Pages:** The pages are thin, almost translucent parchment that look fragile but are oddly resistant to tears and the passage of time. They exude a faint, sweet scent of dried flowers and rot.

- **Ink:** The ink has a dark reddish‑brown hue. A character who examines the book closely and succeeds on an Intelligence (Investigation) check, DC 15, realizes it was written with blood that magically never fully dries, remaining slightly damp.

## Language and Script

- **Language:** The tome is written entirely in **Abyssal**. Reading it requires _comprehending languages_ or fluency in the language.

- **Calligraphy:** The writing is a work of insane artistry. The penwork is elegant and fluid, yet erratic, alternating between precise strokes and frantic scrawls that denote the author’s mental instability. Even with magical aid, the chaotic nature of the script may require an Intelligence (Investigation) check, DC 13, to decipher more complex passages or specific rituals. Failing the check can lead to dangerous misinterpretations of the text.

## Magical Protections

The tome is not an inert object—it has its own defenses:

- **Subtle Madness Aura:** Opening the tome for the first time forces the reader to make a Wisdom saving throw, DC 15. On a failure, the character suffers the effects of a **short‑duration madness** (see the *Dungeon Master’s Guide* table).

## **Lunacy of the Tome of Lutheria (d10)**

| d10 | Thematic Madness Effect |
| :-- | :----------------------- |
| **1** | **Uncontrollable Ecstasy:** The character is seized by savage, unsettling joy. They must use their Action each turn to dance, sing loudly, or laugh hysterically. Movement is allowed, but no Bonus Actions or Reactions. |
| **2** | **Ménade’s Fury:** The character is consumed by blind, irrational rage. They must use their Action to make a melee attack against the nearest creature, friend or foe. |
| **3** | **Hallucinogenic Perception:** The world turns into a chaotic carnival. The character sees other creatures as animals (horns, goat ears, tails) and hears a constant flute music. They have disadvantage on all Wisdom (Perception) checks. |
| **4** | **Primordial Desire:** The character acts on their strongest impulse at the moment (hunger, lust, greed) without regard for consequences. They may try to steal food from an ally’s pack, improperly kiss an NPC, or grab a shiny trinket. |
| **5** | **Profane Babble:** The character can only speak Abyssal—even if they do not know the language—and whispers Lutheria’s dogmas of liberation and chaos. They cannot communicate coherently with allies. |
| **6** | **Bizarre Fascination:** The character becomes utterly enthralled by an inanimate object (a stone, a torch, their own weapon), seeing transcendental beauty in it. They must use their Action to observe and admire, ignoring everything else around them. |
| **7** | **Festive Paranoia:** The character believes that everyone not smiling or having fun is conspiring against them. They become hostile toward any creature with a neutral or serious expression, accusing it of “ruining the party.” |
| **8** | **Release from Bondage:** The character suddenly despises material possessions and rules. They discard a random item from their inventory (chosen by the DM) and ignore any order or plan that seems “boring” or “restrictive.” |
| **9** | **Satyr Mimicry:** The character starts acting like a playful, wild animal—a goat. They communicate through grunts and may attempt headbutts or vault onto furniture, becoming a comical, unpredictable obstacle. |
| **10** | **Vision of Wild Hunt:** The character is paralyzed for 1 minute, experiencing a brilliant vision of an endless hunt in a surreal forest. They see themselves both as hunter and prey. At the vision’s end, the character is terrified of all creatures for 1 minute. |

- **Whispers in Dreams:** Any creature keeping the tome for more than 24 hours begins to have vivid, disturbing nightmares filled with chaotic parties, wild hunts, and Lutheria’s mocking laughter. At the end of each long rest, the bearer must make a Wisdom saving throw, DC 13. On a failure, they do not benefit from the rest and gain one level of exhaustion.

- **Bond with the Author:** The tome is magically bound to Lutheria. She becomes aware whenever the book is opened by a new creature and can, once per day, attempt to peer into the mind of the current reader, unless they are protected by spells such as _non‑detention_.

## Core Content

1. **The Ménades Ritual (The Delirious Ones):** The central, most detailed chapter.

   - **Philosophy:** Describes the ritual not as a curse but as a “liberation” from the fragile shell of civility, a return to a primal state of ecstasy and fury.
   - **Components:** Outlines the profane ingredients needed: the heart of a poet, the blood of a willingly (or unwillingly) sacrificed satyr, and wine fermented with rare mushrooms (“Deadfoot”) that only grow in places of great tragedy.
   - **Process:** Explains the Abyssal chants that distort the mind, the ritual dances that break body and spirit, and the final act of consuming the flesh of a mortal man to seal the transformation. The ritual to turn men into furious satyrs is similar, focusing on channeling lust and savagery.

2. **Treatises on Necromancy and Souls:**
   - **Philosophical Essays:** Contains Lutheria’s thoughts on the nature of souls, the beauty of decay, and the futility of mortal and divine laws.
   - **Studies:** Details experiments on imprisoning souls in objects, corrupting spirits, and using the energy of death to fuel magic. Includes notes on creating the first satyrs.

3. **The Art of Dreams and Nightmares:**
   - **Techniques:** Describes methods to invade mortal dreams, plant suggestions, create vivid nightmares, and extract secrets from sleeping minds.
   - **Exploring Fear:** Contains studies on the primal fears of mortal races and how to exploit them to drive targets to madness or submission.

## Spells Included in the Tome

The tome functions as a warlock’s grimoire (**Book of Shadows**) or a wizard’s spellbook for the spells it contains. An arcane caster can copy the spells into their own spellbook, spending the requisite time and gold.

- **1st Circle:** _Nauseating Ray_
- **2nd Circle:** _Crown of Madness_, _Weakened Ray_
- **3rd Circle:** _Animate Dead_, _Fear_, _Vampiric Touch_
- **4th Circle:** _Maladiction_
- **5th Circle:** _Dream_

## Implications and Adventure Hooks

- **Cultists’ Target:** Followers of Lutheria, especially the Ménades, will sense the tome’s presence and try to recover it at any cost, creating recurring encounters.

- **Lutheria’s Attention:** The Titan may begin haunting the bearer’s dreams, offering power for “small favors,” attempting to corrupt them.

- **A Cure for Madness?:** The transformation ritual is detailed, but the tome may contain hidden clues or marginal notes on how to **reverse** a Ménade’s transformation, offering hope for a NPC—or even one of the party—if tragedy occurs.

- **A Sage’s Bargain:** A reclusive mage or sage at the Mytros Academy may recognize the tome’s power and offer a great reward or important favor in exchange, warning of the dangers of keeping it.
