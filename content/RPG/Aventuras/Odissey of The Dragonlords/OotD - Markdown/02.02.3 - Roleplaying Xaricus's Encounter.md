# Compiled Scenes: Xaricus and the Prophecies of Herkus

This document focuses on the encounter with **Xaricus**, the satyr guardian of the Mossy Temple, and on how Herkus’s prophecies connect to him, serving as the first clue to Demetria’s larger scheme, which has already left the site.

## **Part 1: The Grotesque Guardian – `Xaricus`**

Following the “Demetria Revamped” approach, the dryad is no longer in the temple. In her place she has left one of her most devoted and depraved followers: `Xaricus`, an obese and grotesque satyr who can barely move. He is the “final boss” of the temple, a gluttonous and cruel obstacle.

- **The Scene:** The banquet hall (T15) is in a state of utter profanation. Remnants of food, broken wine jugs, and scattered bones cover the floor. In the center, atop an improvised throne of stacked furniture, lies `Xaricus`. He is a mountain of flesh, with smaller goats bringing him pieces of roasted meat (possibly forest animals, or something worse, depending on your campaign’s tone).

- **Personality:** He is arrogant, gluttonous, and sadistic. He takes pride in having been left behind “to take care of the house.” He lacks the depth of Demetria’s tragedy; he is the pure embodiment of the base corruption she now commands. He addressesia a mix of fear and adoration, calling her “the Queen” or “the Lady of the Vines.”

### **Xaricus’ Interactions with the Epic Paths**

`Xaricus` lacks the subtle awareness of Demetria, but his interactions are more brutal and direct.

#### **Epic Path: The Doomed One** (The‑Doomed One)

- **The Meeting:** `Xaricus` sniffs the air when the Doomed One approaches. He does not understand the nature of the curse, but he recognizes the “mark” of dark power.

- **Feelings:** The Doomed One feels an aura of pure depravity, but without Demetria’s tragic melancholy. `Xaricus` enjoys cruel pleasure, seeing the hero as a broken toy of a higher power.

- **`Xaricus`’ Dialogue (to the Doomed One):**

  > “Hah! I smell the stench of the Lady of Dark Dreams in you! My Queen serves a power that rewards her. You? You’re just the result of a lost bet. She’s gone off to matters more important than dealing with the brood of gods. She’s gone to tear the heart of the forest!”
  > 
  > “Looking for answers? The only answers here are in the depths of a wine glass or the tip of a blade. Your misfortune does not interest me, but your fresh meat… perhaps.”

- **Key Revelation:** `Xaricus`, in his arrogance, reveals that Demetria serves **Lutheria** and that her current plan is to attack druids and the **Heart of the Ancient Forest**. He knows nothing of the Fates, just of **Epic Path: The Haunted One** (The‑Haunted One)

- **The Meeting:** `Xaricus` is wielding the Haunted One’s family artifact as a vulgar ornament, perhaps strapped to his horn or used as a ring on a too‑large finger.

- **Feelings:** The Haunted One feels the attraction to the item, now stained by the satyr’s grease and grime. `Xaricus` sees the item as a trophy, devoid of intrinsic value, a symbol of his victory over the “weak” who tried to oppose Demetria.

- **`Xaricus`’ Dialogue (to the Haunted One):**

  > “Did you like my jewelry? I stole it from a scoundrel who tried to hide the children. They whined about ‘protecting the innocent.’ Fool! The only thing worth protecting is a full belly and a full cup.”
  > 
  > “You want it? You’ll have to take it from me. But I warn you— I’ve already ate satyrs larger than you at breakfast.”

- **Key Revelation:** `Xaricus` reveals that the item was taken from **Loreus**, the satyr who tried to help the children, confirming that Loreus is a potential ally. Recovering the item is the first step for the Haunted One to hear his family’s whispers.

### **The Horn of Balmytria**

You have two options for the Horn, depending on the pacing you desire:

1. **The Horn is with `Xaricus`:** He uses it as his personal goblet, sipping the corrupted wine. This allows the players to recover it here, completing the main objective of the task. `Xaricus` would boast: “The Queen left me her favorite toy while she goes to claim an even greater one!”

2. **Demetria Took the Horn:** `Xaricus` laments that she took the “good wine” with her. “She took the Horn! She said she needed it to water a new garden… a garden of pain for the druids of the Ancient Forest!” This forces the players to chase Demetria to recover the artifact.

## **Part 2: Herkus’s Fragmented Prophecies (Reinterpreted)**

With `Xaricus` in the temple, Herkus’s utterances gain a new context. He is warning about the immediate threat (Xaricus) and the absent threat (Demetria).

| Fragmented Speech | Hidden Meaning (Xaricus Context) |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------|
| “Meeting for the forest not to burn! Goats have horns! Beware the hostess!” | “The hostess” (Demetria) **has already left**. The warning is now about the “horned goats” she left behind, led by the gluttoniest of all (Xaricus). The “meeting” already happened, and the heroes are dealing with its consequences. |
| “Large tree… sick. Dark root drinks black water. Wants to drown the sun.” | The prophecy still hints at the larger plan. The “dark root” (Demetria) has departed to poison the “large tree” (Prosymeina) with the “black water” (the Horn’s wine). Herkus desperately tries to warn that the threat in the temple is merely a symptom of the true disease spreading. |
| “Fat guardian in the stone house. Eats everything. Doesn’t leave a trace. Laughs at the forest.” | A new prophecy, specific to Xaricus. Herkus describes him as a “fat guardian” who devours everything in the temple (“stone house”), mocking the natural order he treasures. It’s a direct warning about the boss awaiting the players in the banquet hall. |
| “The Queen has gone, took the storm with her. Left behind the mud.” | A clear metaphor. The “Queen” (Demetria) is the “storm,” the main and dangerous force. What remains in the temple is the “mud” (Xaricus and the remaining satyrs)—filthy, disgusting, an obstacle, but not the storm itself. |
