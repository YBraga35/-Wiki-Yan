# Deepening the Woodhike Village

This document expands the setting and mission of the **Woodhike Village**, adding layers of depth, complex motivations, and stronger connections to the crisis in Estoria and the overarching plot.

## 1. Woodhike: The Resilient Heart

Woodhike is not merely a tranquil hamlet; it is a bastion of a culture that refuses to die, built upon ancient traditions and a secret that flows from its vines.

### Culture & Tradition

Life in Woodhike is governed by the **“Hill Symphony”**—the belief that every stone, every vine, and every person plays a part in the community’s harmony. Hospitality (*xenia*) is not just a custom; it is the very foundation of its society. Yet the current crisis is causing a dissonance. A faction, the **“Old Guard,”** preaches isolation and distrust of outsiders, while the **“New Generation,”** inspired by the tales of the Heroes, believes salvation lies in opening up to the world.

### The Secret of Golden Cider

Woodhike’s famed cider (or wine) is called **“Sun‑Tears.”** Its legendary quality comes from a single factor: the village’s oldest vines grow in soil that absorbed water from an underground spring, itself nourished by the roots of an ancient tree—one of the lost descendants of a tree that guarded the **Temple of Demeter.** The drink has modest healing properties (restores 1d4 HP once per day) and, for gods like Pythor, evokes memories of a purer, wilder Thylea, before the Pact of Peace. Thus the brew is precious to him; it is a sip of nostalgia and a temporary antidote to his despair.

## 2. Thobos: The Legacy Guardian

Thobos, the Arbón, is more than a worried leader. He carries a secret burden that connects him directly to the threat.

### Motivation & Fear

Thobos’s predecessor, who protected Woodhike during a plague decades ago, made a pact with a shadowy nymph of the Ancient Forest. In exchange for the village’s health, he promised a “gift of hospitality” to anyone who came from the forest in need. Thobos fears that **Korteva**, the bandit leader, is the dark embodiment of that pact coming to collect the debt. He does not ask the Heroes simply to eliminate the bandits; he uses them to try to fix a past mistake he feels powerless to resolve alone, without revealing the truth and triggering panic.

## 3. The Antagonists: The Shadow on the Hill

The villains are more than mere thieves. They are the dark reflection of the crisis that plagues the region.

### Korteva, “The H: Hya” (The “She”)

We unify the names: her name is **Korteva**, and her title among the bandits is **“She,”** which in an ancient dialect means “Hya” (hya meaning *hyena*).

- **Past:** Korteva was not an ordinary bandit. She was a human acolyte of the Order of Sydon, but her faith was radical and cruel even for them. She believed Estoria’s weakness lay in Pythor’s compassion and that only chaos could purify the land. After being expelled and humiliated, she turned to the Titans, seeing their destructive fury as a more honest form of power.
  
- **Motivation:** Her goal is not wealth. She wants to prove that the gods’ order is a sham. She attacks Woodhike not merely for supplies but to break its spirit, to turn its haven of peace into a living example of despair. She wants everyone to see that, in the end, the only law that matters is the law of survival—the law of the hyena. The theft of amphorae and their destruction are symbolic acts to profane the “gift of the gods.”

### The Bandits: The Orphans

Korteva’s band is not composed of heartless killers. Most are **refugees of drought**—farmers who lost everything, desperate families rejected elsewhere. Korteva welcomed them, offering more than food: a purpose and a villain to blame—the “privileged” who still have food, like Woodhike’s residents and Estoria’s citizens. They follow her out of a mix of fear and genuine loyalty.

### Brontes, the Cursed Cyclops

- **Name & Past:** His name is **Brontes** (“Thunder”). He is not a savage beast, but a craftsman, a talented stonecutter who lived in seclusion on the hill. He saw beauty in order and creation.
  
- **Capture:** Korteva did not defeat him by force. She deceived him, using a **fragment of titan amber**, an artifact that whispers promises of power, to convince him he could “awaken the stone’s soul.” Once he touched the amber, the artifact bound his will, forcing obedience. The chains that bind him are secondary; his true prison is magical. He despises the destruction he is forced to cause, and his mind is a constant battle against the amber’s influence.

## 4. The Mission: More Than Stolen Wine

The mission unfolds in stages, offering investigation, combat, and a moral dilemma.

### Stage 1: Investigation

Instead of following footprints, the Heroes can find richer clues:

- A survivor of the attack may describe not only the bandits’ cruelty but also their desperation, noting that one of them wept for his family.
  
- Near a broken amphora, they may find a strange symbol carved into the earth: a Titan’s profane sigil, hinting at Korteva’s true affiliation.
  
- In Woodhike, an elder of the **Old Guard** may, reluctantly, talk about the “pact” of the old arbón, giving the Heroes a clue to the supernatural nature of the threat.

### Stage 2: Hideout & Moral Dilemma

Korteva’s hideout is not a fortress, but a makeshift, grim camp in a cave or ruin. Heroes will see the bandits’ families, starving children. They will realize that a frontal attack would spark a massacre of desperate people. Korteva and her elite warriors live apart in a more fortified area, forcing the players to choose:

- **Full Assault:** Ignore the context and wipe out the threat, efficient but morally troubling.
  
- **Stealth Action:** Infiltrate the camp to neutralise Korteva and free Brontes, hoping the rest of the band will surrender.
  
- **Negotiation:** Attempt dialogue, offering the bandits an alternative (perhaps an accord with Thobos), risking alerting Korteva.

### Stage 3: Confrontation and Liberation

The final battle is against Korteva and her most loyal lieutenants. She fights with fanatical ferocity, using dirty tactics and mocking the Heroes’ ideals. During the fight, Brontes is forced to act as a “living siege weapon.”

To free the cyclops, Heroes must destroy the titan amber Korteva wields in a necklace or staff, not just break his physical chains. Once free, Brontes may either turn against his captors in righteous fury or remain stunned, depending on the desired drama.

## 5. Connections & Consequences

Actions in Woodhike should echo in Estoria and beyond.

- **The King and the Cider:** Saving Woodhike and restoring the “Sun‑Tear” supply can give Pythor a moment of clarity, a flashback to his former self. He may grant the Heroes a significant boon, perhaps revealing a weakness in an enemy or permitting access to a royal vault.
  
- **The Fate of the Orphans:** If the Heroes find a peaceful solution for the bandits (perhaps persuading Thobos to offer refuge in exchange for labor), they earn a network of loyal informants. This could prove crucial later, offering clues on Titan forces’ movements.
  
- **A Stone & Thunder Ally:** If Brontes is rescued, he becomes a powerful ally. Grateful, he may offer his services to fortify Estoria, forge a unique weapon for a Hero, or—most importantly—use his stone‑connection to reveal a hidden titan ruin or a secret about the Rock of Estor.
  
- **The Mirror of Estoria:** Resolving the conflict in Woodhike functions as a thematic microcosm. Choosing compassion over annihilation shows the Heroes that strength lies not only in the sword, but in the ability to forge peace amid despair—an urgent lesson that Estoria and all of Thylea must learn.
