# Divine Power at the Mortal Court

Use after the audience if the heroes persuade Vallus to act with her divine siblings against Sydon’s storm.

## The Gods Arrive

> The rain’s hammering ceases in an instant. A deep silence falls upon the Throne Hall. A single musical note — pure and crystalline — echoes from nowhere and everywhere. The doors swing open. **Kyrah**, Goddess of Music, enters with a noble sorrow in her eyes, her lyre’s melody trailing her like a mantle.
>
> The air crackles with ozone and the scent of polished bronze. A wavering heat‑haze coalesces into **Pythor**, God of Battle — a warrior‑king in a simple tunic, a sheathed blade at his side. A storm contained.
>
> A wave of dry heat — like a forge’s breath — sweeps the hall. From shadows behind the throne emerges **Volkan**, God of the Forge: shorter than the others, yet mountain‑steady, eyes gleaming with an ancient hearth’s light.

## The Miracle Rite

> **Volkan** strikes the floor; a low hum rises from the palace foundations, climbing columns like sap through an ancient tree.
>
> **Pythor** raises a hand; an invisible aura of martial will emanates, a bulwark against the Titan’s fury.
>
> **Kyrah**’s melody swells, weaving a counter‑song that resolves the hurricane’s dissonance into peace.
>
> **Vallus** closes her eyes; threads of silver light extend from her to the others, guiding and interlacing their power into a single working.
>
> Outside, a dome of golden, silver, and bronze light blossoms over Mytros. Sydon’s face roars in frustrated silence before unraveling. The downpour becomes a drizzle — then stops. A sunbeam pierces the clouds.

## Aftermath

> Kyrah grows pale; Pythor leans upon his sheathed sword; Volkan seems older; Vallus stands composed but fragile. The miracle is done — and a price paid.
